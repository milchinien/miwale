Trick or Treat — Game Art Style Spec

A glossy Halloween candy world: hard-candy shine, gummy jelly, matte marshmallow and chocolate on a melting chocolate floor.

Everything in this world is edible and rendered like a premium candy commercial. Each body part is a path filled with a radial gradient lit from the top-left, then given clipped "material passes": a saturated jelly edge, a warm inner glow, ribs or stripes, a lower-right rim light, and sharp white speculars. The finished sprite gets one soft cocoa-plum silhouette outline and nothing else. Below the characters lies a milk-chocolate bar floor with sunken caramel pools, pink strawberry sugar-sand, raised mint frosting with dripping glossy edges and dark cookie-crumb paths, all sprinkled with sprinkles. In the module's example cast the main character has a round hard-candy orange head on a licorice body; the creatures are two-layer gummy root bulbs, matte marshmallow floating sheets and a large dark-chocolate figure with glowing caramel cracks.

When to use it

  • Good fits: cute top-down action with many figures, casual arcade shooters, match-3 or puzzle games with characters, seasonal/holiday events, kids-friendly action, anything with "collect lots of shiny stuff".
  • Why it works: the glossy material reads as "valuable / delicious", so collectibles and bursts feel rewarding; the warm ground lets saturated sprites pop; sprinkles make every action feel like a celebration.
  • Bad fits: grim, realistic or horror-serious tones (it is always cheeky); retro low-colour games; dense strategy UIs that need calm neutrals.
  • Risk: with too many hues and effects at once it turns into "candy chaos". Groups of 200+ figures still read because every figure shares the same silhouette treatment and outline.

Palette rules

  • Every material is a triple (light / mid / dark) used inside one radial gradient. No body is a flat fill.
  • Highlights are pure white. Shadows, outlines and vignette are warm cocoa-plum — never black, never grey, never blue.
  • Ground ≈ mid value, warm and slightly desaturated; characters are fully saturated with white speculars → strong figure/ground separation.
  • Glow ramp for everything lit from inside: #fffbd6 → #ffe07a (35%) → #ffa12a (75%) → #e2650e.
  • Creature variants change only the gummy hue; shared white bottom layer and green jelly leaves keep the family readable.

Rendering technique

  1. Units. The camera shows 360 world units vertically; S = screen px per unit. Sprite bake resolution K = clamp(round(S·1.25), 3, 6) px per unit.
  2. Bake at init (build(box, fn, opt)): create a canvas for the world-space box + 1-unit margin, scale(K), translate(-left, -top), round joins/caps, run the part-drawing function, then add the outline: make a solid copy tinted #3a1424 (source-atop), stamp it 12 times (16 if the outline is > 2.5 px) on a circle of radius ol·K px, draw the original on top. Default ol = 0.5 units.
  3. Per character: 4 walk frames × {normal, flash-tinted #fff3f8, mirrored, mirrored flash} (set4). The main character also has a 4-frame alternative-face set. Marshmallow figures get a destination-in gradient fade at the hem (alpha 1 at y = −8 → 0.35 at y = 1).
  4. Part drawing order inside a sprite (back to front): back accessories (scarf tail, leaves) → back arm → legs → body → front details → front arm → collar/scarf → head → face → speculars → leaf on top.
  5. Material passes per part: (a) gloss radial fill; (b) clipped passes (clipDo): jelly edge, inner glow, stripes, ribs, grooves; (c) rim lower-right light; (d) spec white ellipses.
  6. Ground: per-pixel material compositing into 256-unit chunks (generator, yields every few rows), max 2.5 px/unit; decals stamped afterwards at 4 px/unit.
  7. Per frame: ground chunks → ground overlay (wrapper decals) → collectibles → shadows → y-sorted props/creatures/main character → orbiting objects → projectiles → particles → numbers → world overlay (drifting sugar sparkles, additive) → screen overlay (vignette).
  8. All dynamic motion (squash, bob, tilt, flash) is applied at blit time through the transform; nothing is re-rendered per frame.

World and ground

The floor is computed per pixel from noise fields sampled on a 4-unit grid and bilinearly interpolated; each material layer is a signed field (positive = inside) whose value divided by its gradient gives a distance in pixels → perfectly antialiased edges plus distance-based shading. The ground stays mid-value and warm so the saturated figures pop.

  • Milk chocolate (base, #7e4c32): where a low-frequency mask (smoothstep(.44,.58, fbm(x/620))) is on, the floor is moulded into rows of bars. Rows are 34 / 26 / 34 / 30 units high (repeating every 124), each row shifted by its own hashed offset like brickwork, bars 34 units wide, every bar with its own tone (±3.5 %), so no square grid repeats. Groove < 0.9 units darkens up to −26 %; top/left 3.2-unit bevel +11 %; bottom/right bevel −9 %; a faint +2.5 % dome at 40 % of the bar. Where the mask is off, the chocolate "melts": sine gloss bands sin(phase + x·0.018 + y·0.062) > 0.6 brighten up to +9 %, < −0.7 darken. Global tint ±4.5 % from very low-frequency noise.
  • Caramel pools (sunken): colour from #ec9c3a at the edge to #b25a1a 0.05 field units inside. Thin glossy streaks where sin(y·0.2 + x·0.035 + warp) > 0.955 lerp toward rgb(255,232,170). Upper inner edge darkens (−38% falloff over 4 units: the lip casts shadow into the pool); lower inner edge gets a light lip rgb(255,226,160) between 0.7 and 2 units in. Outside the pool, the chocolate brightens 12% within 1.3 units.
  • Pink sugar-sand #f4acc2: per 0.5-unit texel hash grain ±3.5%, 0.3% of texels pure #fff6fa sparkle; drop shadow (rgb 60,22,30) up to 28% within 2.6 units, stronger on down-facing edges; top edges +12% lighter, bottom edges −12%, very edge −12%.
  • Mint frosting #a0dab8 (raised): piped ridges sin((y + warp + x·0.16)·0.62) ±4.5%, ridge crests lerp to white. Down-facing edges grow drips (every 4.2 units, 62% chance, up to 9 units long, parabolic profile). Shadow below up to 50% over 5 units. A white gloss line 0.45–1.35 units inside up-facing edges (75%), top edges +16% lighter, bottom −22%, last 0.6 units −16%.
  • Cookie-crumb paths #543226: thin noise ridge |perlin| < 0.0155 as a winding band; texture = 128-texel tileable Worley cells (0.5 units per texel), each cell domed and lit from the top-left (0.78 + 0.22·edge ± light); edge displaced by fBm for a crumbly outline; −20% at the edge and a sunken shadow on the upper side.
  • Decals are spills, not a sprinkle (one candidate every 15 units; each with an rgba(50,15,20,.25) ellipse shadow offset (0.3, 0.4)):
    • A spill field smoothstep(0.56, 0.78, noise(x/140)) decides where candy lies, as if a bag burst there. Chocolate chips, candy corn, mini marshmallows and fans of 4 sprinkles appear only inside spills; the rest of the chocolate stays calm bars.
    • Sugar sand keeps its sparkle grains everywhere (8 %, the material itself). Hearts, gumdrops and mini lollipops appear only in spills.
    • Mint frosting gets a few piped dollops, plus gumdrops and sprinkles in spills.
    • Caramel gets sparse star glints, and the cookie path is made of crumbs and chips.
    • Edges: crumbs spill off the cookie path onto the chocolate, and sugar glints line the frosting rims.

Characters

All figures are chunky toy shapes built from glossy primitives; each part gets the gloss recipe and a material pass, and the whole sprite gets one soft cocoa outline (0.5 units). Faces either glow caramel (carved holes) or sparkle (glossy dark eyes with yellow and white catchlights). The material encodes the figure type.

Props

All props use the same gloss recipe (top-left radial, white specs, lower-right rim) with a 0.45-unit cocoa outline (lollipops and cupcake houses 0.55), a warm soft shadow sized per prop, and are y-sorted with the characters. They are chosen to match the ground material they stand on.

  • Lollipop trees: stick 40 units, ribbon bow, swirl disc r 13.5 with spiral bands, inner shading, rim and 3 specs.
  • Gumdrop bushes: 3 sugared gumdrops with 26 crystal dots.
  • Candy-cane fence: red licorice rails, striped posts with dashed hooks.
  • Wrapped-candy boulders.
  • Chocolate gravestones with piped frosting "R.I.P." and a bead border.
  • Candy buckets shaped like orange gourds.
  • Cupcake houses: pleated liner, 3-tier frosting roof, sprinkles, cherry, warm windows.

Placement is composed (34-unit jittered grid):

  • Lollipop groves: a grove field fbm(x/360) > 0.63 holds lollipop trees (36 %) and gumdrop bushes. The lollipop colour depends on the ground: mint/teal swirls on frosting, pink on sugar, orange/purple on chocolate. A ring of gumdrop bushes forms each grove's rim (0.585–0.63).
  • The cookie path: lined with candy-cane fences (16 % of the edge cells) and wrapped-candy boulders.
  • Little graveyards: gravestones and candy buckets grouped where a graveyard field peaks on the chocolate.
  • Elsewhere: the odd cupcake house on the frosting or sugar; open floor stays calm. Nothing stands on caramel or on the path.

Ambient motion: lollipops sway on their sticks (base-anchored skew sin(1.1t)·0.03 + sin(2.3t)·0.01), and gumdrop bushes jiggle (±2.5 % at 2.6 rad/s). Each prop has its own phase.

Effects and particles

All particles are baked sprites with the same outline rule; only sparkles are additive.

  • Projectile: glossy candy seed (gloss + 1.6×0.55 spec) squashing on Y with |cos(spin)|; white gradient streak 13 units long, 1.6 wide, plus two pink side streaks 0.6 wide.
  • Impact: 8-point rounded star r 5.6/2.4, white → #ffb3d6, hot-pink outline 0.35; life 0.14 s; 50% chance of a sprinkle and a sugar sparkle.
  • Sprinkles: capsules 2.3×0.9 with white top highlight and dark bottom line, 7 colours, vz 50–120, gravity 300, drag 1.4, spin ±8 rad/s.
  • Pop-out (disappearing): sprinkles (8 / 16 for the large creature), gummy chunks (6 blobs in the creature colour + green + white), chocolate shards + caramel drops + candy corn + cream puffs + shock ring for the large creature, marshmallow puffs (5-lobe cloud) for floating ones.
  • Collectibles: wrapped candies — glossy body, 3-arm spiral swirl (width 0.28r), cellophane ends with white crease lines, spec at (−0.38r, −0.42r). Small = blue #2fb8ff r 2.4, large = orange #ffb11f with pink swirl r 3.6.
  • Orbiting object: a small two-layer gummy bulb (cream bottom, purple top) with a caramel-hole face and an additive glow.
  • Dash: 3 flash-tinted afterimages at 7-unit steps (alpha 0.4/i), cream puffs and sugar sparkles.
  • Wrapper reveal (appearing): unwrapping candy-wrapper decal under the figure (outBack in, fades after 70%), confetti; the figure scales in with outElastic.
  • Celebration: expanding ring (cocoa, white, dashed pink), 22 sprinkles, 14 confetti that flip (Y-scale by cos) and 8 sparkles.
  • Sugar sparkle: 4-point star (quadratic curves, tip 3.6) over a coloured soft glow, lighter blend.

Atmosphere and lighting

  • Key light: top-left, everywhere (gradient centre offset −0.38r, −0.45r). Speculars are always upper-left; rim light lower-right.
  • Shadows: a 64×32 baked radial ellipse rgba(45,12,22,.42) → .3 @55% → 0, stretched per figure: main character 9×3.2 (shrinking while airborne), small creature 9×3.2, floating creature 6.2×2.2 at 70%, large creature 20×6, collectibles 3.2×1.3, projectiles 2.6×1 offset 6 down. Warm cocoa, never grey or blue.
  • Glows: additive radial glow sprite (36 units) behind the main character's head and lanterns; shadowBlur glow baked into caramel cracks and glowing eyes.
  • Ambient sparkles: one per 140-unit cell (60% of cells), drifting and twinkling, additive.
  • Vignette: radial from (50%, 46%) inner r 0.35h to outer 0.62w: rgba(60,10,50,0) → .16 @70% → rgba(50,8,40,.42). Plum, not black.
  • Overlays: full-screen announcement overlays tint rgba(70,20,60,.62) with a pink glow and a rotating sunburst; a sombre end-screen variant desaturates.

Motion and animation

Smooth, interpolated motion (a rendered material, not drawn on twos). Everything that is jelly rings on after it moves: a damped oscillation follows every contact, every hit and every landing.

  • Main character jelly hop: phase = anim·0.42 hops, f = fract(phase).
    • 0–0.25 landing: a squash that rings on as a damped wobble, jig = e^(−14f)·cos(28f) (sy −15 %, sx +13 %).
    • 0.25–0.38 crouch (anticipation): squash 9 % and a back tilt.
    • 0.38–1 airborne: air = sin(π(f − 0.38)/0.62), lift 3.4·air, stretching with vertical speed and slightly squashed at the apex. The airborne frame is 2; landings alternate frames 1/3.
    • Tilt 0.07·facing·air. A sugar sparkle puff on 60 % of landings.
  • Idle: breathing sy = 1 + .03·sin(3.2t), sx = 1 − .022·sin(3.2t), frame 0.
  • Action squash: 5 %. Celebration wobble: sin(T·4π)·T (sy ±18 %). Flash: sx +16 %, sy −13 %, flash overlay ≤ 0.75, alternative face; blinking at 16 Hz, alpha 0.5.
  • Gummy creatures: phase anim·0.85 + seed, 4 frames, bob 1.6, rotation 0.09·sin. Every contact sets off a jiggle that rings on after the step (e^(−9f)·cos(24f), sx +10 %, sy −11 %), so the jelly visibly lags the gait.
  • Marshmallow floating creatures: 6 fps frame cycle, hover 4.5 + 1.6·sin(2.6t), squash ±5 % at 4.2 rad/s, tilt by velocity.
  • Large creature: phase anim·0.32, cubic contact squash 6 % plus a slower jiggle (e^(−7f)·cos(18f), 5 %), bob 2.
  • Hit (~0.5 s): a jelly spring e^(−8t)·cos(30t) (±22 %: squash → stretch → settle) with a 0.26 rad tilt away from the blow, and the flash overlay.
  • Removal (deathT 0 → 1, 0.35 s in motion):
    • Gummy pop: the creature wobbles (±13 %, 70 rad/s), over-inflates to 1.38× and stretches upward while the flash copy rises to 95 %. At 0.34 it pops, and the layered burst (sprinkles, gummy chunks, sugar sparkles, puff) takes over.
    • Whipped cream: the floating creature sags (height −70 %), spreads (+55 %), slides 4.5 units down into a puddle and fades by 0.85.
  • Appearing: the wrapper decal unwraps under the figure; gummies scale in with outElastic, marshmallows with outBack.
  • Collectibles: bob 1.2 ± 0.9 at 4 rad/s, sway ±0.18, squash 5 %; pop with outBack (0.4 → 1) and an arc of 10 units.
  • Numbers: scale 0.3 → 1.35 (10 %) → 1.0 (24 %), rise 4 units, slight random tilt ±0.15 rad.

Do and don't

Do

  • Fill every shape with the top-left radial gradient (light → mid @ 0.5 → dark) and add a white specular ellipse at about (−0.4r, −0.45r).
  • Add lower-right rim light to round glossy forms; jelly edge bands to gummies; inner glow to hard candy.
  • Pick a real candy material per object and keep it consistent (it also encodes the creature type).
  • Wrap each finished sprite in one 0.5-unit #3a1424 outline.
  • Make faces glow (caramel holes) or sparkle (dark glossy eyes with yellow + white catchlights).
  • Reward everything with sprinkles, sparkles and confetti.
  • Keep shadows warm, soft and elliptical.

Don't

  • Don't use flat fills or hard cel bands — it becomes generic vector cartoon.
  • Don't use black outlines or ink lines inside shapes.
  • Don't vary the light direction between objects.
  • Don't use grey/blue shadows or a black vignette.
  • Don't make the ground as saturated as the characters.
  • Don't add new random hues; extend existing triples and the sprinkle set.
  • Don't rebuild gradients every frame per figure — bake.

Implementation recipe

  1. Set up a world of 360 units visible height; compute S = canvasHeight / 360 and K = clamp(round(S·1.25), 3, 6).
  2. Write the helpers: gloss, spec, softSpec, rim, clipDo, jelly, caramelHole, shape builders (E, CAP, roundPoly, blobPath, turnipPath/your silhouette, leafPath), and build() with the 12-stamp outline.
  3. Define materials as colour triples: hard candy, licorice, gummy (per variant hi/top/dk + edge + glow), marshmallow, chocolate, jelly leaf.
  4. Design the main character head-heavy: a big glossy head (≈ 22×18 units) on a small dark body (≈ 9 units) with bright accents (scarf, shoes). Draw back-to-front, finishing with speculars.
  5. Build each creature type from one material; give it 4 frames by moving feet/leaves slightly; bake normal, flash-tinted and mirrored sprites.
  6. Bake collectibles (wrapped candies), projectiles (candy seed + streak), particles (sprinkles, gummy chunks, puffs, impact star, sparkle) and the shadow sprite.
  7. Build the ground: chocolate base with bar grid, then noise-driven material regions (caramel sunken, sugar flat with grain, frosting raised with drips and gloss line, cookie paths). Cache in chunks; stamp decals with tiny shadows.
  8. Place props by feature, not by a flat chance per material: lollipop groves with gumdrop rims, cane fences along the cookie path, little graveyards, the odd cupcake house; y-sort them with the characters.
  9. Per frame: blit sprites with squash/stretch/tilt transforms, additive glows behind light sources, particles, floating numbers, ambient sparkles, plum vignette.
  10. Style the HUD with the CSS above: cocoa borders + base drop, gloss overlay, peppermint bars, Chewy numbers with the 16-direction outline.

A complete runnable kit (main character, small gummy creature, floating marshmallow creature, lollipop, wrapped candies, chocolate/caramel/sugar/mint/cookie ground, impact effect) is in starter.js; the full reference is ../../js/styles/candy.js.

// Material core: top-left lit radial fill, white specular, lower-right rim light.
const TAU = Math.PI * 2;
function rg(c, x0, y0, r0, x1, y1, r1, stops) { const g = c.createRadialGradient(x0, y0, r0, x1, y1, r1); for (const s of stops) g.addColorStop(s[0], s[1]); return g; }
function lg(c, x0, y0, x1, y1, stops) { const g = c.createLinearGradient(x0, y0, x1, y1); for (const s of stops) g.addColorStop(s[0], s[1]); return g; }
function gloss(c, path, cx, cy, r, light, mid, dark) {
  c.beginPath(); path(c);
  c.fillStyle = rg(c, cx - r * 0.38, cy - r * 0.45, r * 0.05, cx, cy, r * 1.18, [[0, light], [0.5, mid], [1, dark]]);
  c.fill();
}
function spec(c, x, y, rx, ry, rot, a = 0.9) {
  c.save(); c.globalAlpha = a; c.fillStyle = '#fff';
  c.beginPath(); c.ellipse(x, y, rx, ry, rot, 0, TAU); c.fill(); c.restore();
}
function clipDo(c, path, fn) { c.save(); c.beginPath(); path(c); c.clip(); fn(c); c.restore(); }
function rim(c, path, x0, y0, x1, y1, col, w) {          // stroke clipped inside the path
  clipDo(c, path, (k) => {
    k.lineWidth = w;
    k.strokeStyle = lg(k, x0, y0, x1, y1, [[0, 'rgba(255,255,255,0)'], [0.6, 'rgba(255,255,255,0)'], [1, col]]);
    k.beginPath(); path(k); k.stroke();
  });
}

// Gummy / jelly: milky core + saturated inner edge band + subsurface glow lower-right.
function jelly(c, path, cx, cy, r, edgeCol, glowCol) {
  clipDo(c, path, (k) => {
    k.fillStyle = rg(k, cx - r * 0.1, cy - r * 0.05, r * 0.1, cx, cy, r * 1.1,
      [[0, 'rgba(255,255,255,0.28)'], [0.55, 'rgba(255,255,255,0.06)'], [1, 'rgba(255,255,255,0)']]);
    k.fillRect(cx - r * 2, cy - r * 2, r * 4, r * 4);
    k.lineWidth = r * 0.42; k.strokeStyle = edgeCol;          // half of it is clipped away -> 0.21r band
    k.beginPath(); path(k); k.stroke();
    if (glowCol) {
      k.fillStyle = rg(k, cx + r * 0.42, cy + r * 0.45, 0, cx + r * 0.42, cy + r * 0.45, r * 0.7, [[0, glowCol], [1, 'rgba(255,255,255,0)']]);
      k.fillRect(cx - r * 2, cy - r * 2, r * 4, r * 4);
    }
  });
}
// purple gummy: gloss(c, body, 0, -12, 10.5, '#d9a2ff', '#9443d8', '#56168f');
//               jelly(c, body, 0, -12, 10.5, 'rgba(80,10,140,0.55)', 'rgba(255,170,255,0.55)');
// Carved face hole glowing with caramel: dark fill, then the same path shifted (0.2, 0.55) with the glow ramp.
function caramelHole(c, path, bx, by, br) {
  c.beginPath(); path(c); c.fillStyle = '#7a2406'; c.fill();
  clipDo(c, path, (k) => {
    k.translate(0.2, 0.55);
    k.beginPath(); path(k);
    k.fillStyle = rg(k, bx, by, 0, bx, by, br, [[0, '#fffbd6'], [0.35, '#ffe07a'], [0.75, '#ffa12a'], [1, '#e2650e']]);
    k.fill();
  });
}
// Bake a sprite at k px/unit and add the soft cocoa silhouette outline (12 stamped tinted copies).
function build(box, k, drawFn, ol = 0.5, olc = '#3a1424') {
  const m = 1, l = box[0] - m, t = box[1] - m;
  const c = document.createElement('canvas');
  c.width = Math.ceil((box[2] + m - l) * k); c.height = Math.ceil((box[3] + m - t) * k);
  const x = c.getContext('2d');
  x.scale(k, k); x.translate(-l, -t); x.lineJoin = 'round'; x.lineCap = 'round';
  drawFn(x);
  const tint = document.createElement('canvas'); tint.width = c.width; tint.height = c.height;
  const tx = tint.getContext('2d');
  tx.drawImage(c, 0, 0); tx.globalCompositeOperation = 'source-atop'; tx.fillStyle = olc; tx.fillRect(0, 0, c.width, c.height);
  const o = document.createElement('canvas'); o.width = c.width; o.height = c.height;
  const ox = o.getContext('2d'), w = ol * k, n = w > 2.5 ? 16 : 12;
  for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) { const a = (i / n) * TAU; ox.drawImage(tint, Math.cos(a) * w, Math.sin(a) * w); }
  ox.drawImage(c, 0, 0);
  return { c: o, ox: -l * k, oy: -t * k, k };   // anchor = world origin (feet)
}
function blit(ctx, s, x, y, sx = 1, sy = 1) {
  const q = 1 / s.k;
  ctx.drawImage(s.c, x - s.ox * q * sx, y - s.oy * q * sy, s.c.width * q * sx, s.c.height * q * sy);
}
// Main character jelly hop (applied at blit time, sprites stay baked): landing squash that rings on,
// a crouch before take-off, stretch while airborne.
function heroPose(anim, moving, t) {
  if (!moving) { const b = Math.sin(t * 3.2); return { frame: 0, lift: 0, sx: 1 - 0.022 * b, sy: 1 + 0.03 * b, rot: 0 }; }
  const ph = anim * 0.42, f = ph - Math.floor(ph), hop = Math.floor(ph);
  const air = f > 0.38 ? Math.sin(Math.PI * (f - 0.38) / 0.62) : 0;
  const jig = Math.exp(-14 * f) * Math.cos(f * 28);                         // damped jelly wobble after landing
  const crouch = f > 0.25 && f < 0.38 ? Math.sin(Math.PI * (f - 0.25) / 0.13) : 0;
  const vel = f > 0.38 ? Math.abs(Math.cos(Math.PI * (f - 0.38) / 0.62)) : 0;
  return { frame: air > 0.05 ? 2 : (hop & 1 ? 1 : 3), lift: air * 3.4, rot: 0.07 * air - 0.03 * crouch,
           sx: 1 + 0.13 * jig + 0.07 * crouch - 0.08 * vel * air, sy: 1 - 0.15 * jig - 0.09 * crouch + 0.12 * vel * air };
}
// Any jelly hit: a damped spring (t = seconds since the hit).
const jellySpring = (t) => { const w = Math.exp(-8 * t) * Math.cos(t * 30); return { sx: 1 + 0.22 * w, sy: 1 - 0.22 * w }; };

Adapting it to your project

  • Keep fixed: the top-left gradient recipe, white speculars, lower-right rim, the single cocoa outline, warm cocoa shadows/vignette, sprinkle rewards, Chewy + Sniglet, the "material = figure type" idea.
  • May change: the theme (any confectionery or toy world: Christmas candy, ice cream, bakery), the material set (add ice-cream scoop = matte with drip, fondant = flat-ish matte with sugar crystals, glass candy = jelly with stronger transparency), the ground materials (e.g. snow-sugar, jam rivers) — as long as they stay mid-value and calmer than the figures.
  • Palette swap: replace a triple as a whole (light ≈ mid mixed 55% with white, dark ≈ mid × 0.6 — the module's own gumdrop() rule). Keep the outline/shadow hue warm and dark; a cool theme may shift it to a deep plum-blue but never pure black.
  • New characters: start from a silhouette path, choose a material, add 1 main spec + 1 dot spec + 1 weak secondary reflection, then a glowing or glossy face. Large creatures: bigger outline (0.6), glowing cracks with shadowBlur.
  • New props: pick a confection that matches the ground material, apply the same gloss recipe, 0.45 outline and a warm soft shadow.
  • Other genres: platformers — keep the same lighting but use a side-view ground made from the same materials (chocolate blocks with bar bevels, frosting tops with drips). Puzzle games — tiles are wrapped candies / gumdrops with the same gloss.
  • Resolution: keep K between 3 and 6 px/unit; below 3 the speculars blur away, above 6 memory grows with little gain.

Performance notes

  • Everything except the ground is baked once: per character 4 frames × 4 variants (normal, flash, mirrored ×2); per-frame cost is drawImage with a transform per figure (blitRot sets the transform directly instead of save/restore).
  • The ground is the expensive part: noise fields sampled every 4 units, per-pixel material compositing, run as a generator that yields every 4 rows and in 128-row putImageData bands so chunk generation never stalls a frame; resolution capped at 2.5 px/unit (groundResMax). Pixels whose 2×2 cell is fully inside/outside a layer skip the distance math.
  • Decals are stamped into the chunk once (≤ 1 per 15 units, most of them only inside spills). The bar rows add two hashes per chocolate pixel at chunk build time.
  • Additive glows (lighter) are limited to the main character's halo, lanterns, sugar sparkles; shadowBlur is only used at bake time.
  • Floating numbers are cached as sprites per value (cache cleared above 400 entries); the vignette is a 320-px sprite stretched to the screen.
  • For a simpler port, the vector ground in starter.js (pre-baked once per canvas size) costs < 1 ms per frame.
