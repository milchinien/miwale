Pop-Art Comic — Game Art Style Spec

A printed 1960s comic book come alive: brush-ink outlines, flat process colours, Ben-Day dots and slipped colour plates.

Everything on screen looks like cheap four-colour newsprint. Sprites are flat process colours with thick, slightly lopsided black brush outlines; volume comes only from halftone dots (bigger dots = darker) in a crescent on the shadow side plus a solid spot-black rim; a cyan or magenta colour plate peeks out from behind each figure because the "printing press" is out of register. The ground is warm paper divided into big organic regions, each with its own rotated dot screen and separated by inked contour lines. Big moments explode into jagged yellow starbursts with POW!/ZONK! balloons. The look is loud, punchy and spooky-fun, like a 60s horror comic for kids.

When to use it

Good fits:

  • Busy scenes with many figures — saturated characters on pale dotted paper stay readable even with 200 creatures on screen.
  • Projects with lots of visual feedback — impacts, bursts and celebrations map naturally to comic starbursts and sound words.
  • Halloween, superhero, B-movie, pulp themes — the 60s horror-comic print language is instantly recognisable.
  • UI-heavy projects (cards, shops, menus) — comic panels with hard shadows make crisp, characterful UI.

Bad fits:

  • Atmospheric or realistic horror — there are no soft lights, no darkness, no gradients.
  • Calm cozy projects — the style shouts; it is hard to make it quiet.
  • Strict retro pixel art — this is vector-smooth with anti-aliasing, not a pixel grid.
  • Mostly-dark scenes — paper is light by definition; a "night" region is pale blue paper with blue dots, not black.

Palette rules:

  • Every fill has a darker dot sister used only for halftone shade. Never shade by darkening the fill.
  • Black is always warm ink #1b1620, never #000.
  • Ground = pale paper tints with mid-saturation dots. Characters = full-saturation process colours. This value gap is what keeps crowds readable.
  • Plates are only cyan #2ec4ef (solid characters, bursts) or magenta #e0338f (ghosts, collectibles, projectiles) at 60–65 % alpha; props use pink #ff3d6e at 45 %.
  • Complementary dot overlays: cyan dots on magenta, magenta on violet, yellow on magenta, red on yellow.
  • No gradients, no blur, no glow — tone is dot size or dot density.

Typography

Bangers 400 — https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bangers — sound words, pop-up numbers, HUD counters, titles, card names. Fallback stack: "Bangers", "Luckiest Guy", Impact, "Arial Black", sans-serif (use bold when falling back).

.big-title  { font: 400 92px/1 'Bangers', Impact, sans-serif; letter-spacing: 4px; color: #ffe23a; transform: rotate(-4deg); }

Comic Neue 700 — https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Comic+Neue — labels, descriptions, hints; often uppercase with 1 px letter-spacing.

.hint { font: 700 16px/1.25 'Comic Neue', 'Comic Sans MS', sans-serif; letter-spacing: 1px; text-transform: uppercase; color: #1b1620; }

Text effects:

  • Ink outline in CSS: 16 hard text-shadows around a circle (cos/sin × w px, 0 blur, #1b1620) plus one drop shadow 8px 8px 0 #1b1620 for big titles (w = 4 for titles, 2 for bar labels, 1.5 for small labels).
  • On canvas: draw a colour-plate copy offset (−0.7, +0.6) units at 85 % alpha (magenta, or cyan for emphasised numbers), then strokeText with lineWidth = 0.22 × font size in ink, then fillText in white/yellow.
  • Emphasised numbers: yellow fill with red halftone dots clipped to the lower half of the letters (source-atop).
  • Sound words get a hard drop shadow of ink offset (+0.9, +0.9) units before the stroke.

Rendering technique

  1. Choose bake resolution K = clamp(round(screenHeightPx / 360 × 1.4), 3, 5) pixels per world unit. The camera shows 360 world units vertically.
  2. Bake every sprite once with the part painter (see Code). For each part, in order:
    1. clip() to the part shape;
    2. flat fill (or a two-tone fill function);
    3. optional Ben-Day overlay dots: [colour, rf, spacing, rotationDeg] across the whole part;
    4. shade crescent: clip to "everything minus the shape shifted by sh" (even-odd) and fill with dots in the dark sister colour (rf 0.45–0.62). sh points toward the light (negative x/y), so the crescent appears on the bottom-right;
    5. spot-black crescent: same trick with a smaller offset spot (≈ 40 % of sh), filled solid ink;
    6. details (ribs, patterns) and white shine ellipses at top-left;
    7. restore() and stroke the outline twice: width w, then 0.85 w translated by (0.3 w, 0.36 w). The result is a brush line that swells on the bottom-right.
    8. optional interior ink lines at 0.55 × ol (≈ 0.55 units).
  3. Halftone tile: a T×T canvas (T = round(spacing × K), min 3 px) with circles at the 4 corners and the centre (a 45° diamond lattice), radius = rf × T / 2; used as a repeating pattern with pattern.setTransform(DOMMatrix().rotate(deg).scale(1/K)) so the dots live in world units. Sprite dot spacing is 1.3 units (DSP).
  4. Misregistration: after painting, tint a silhouette copy of the sprite with the plate colour and draw it destination-over at alpha 0.65, offset (−0.5 K, +0.375 K) px — i.e. 0.5 units left, 0.375 down.
  5. Variants per figure: 4 animation frames × {normal, flash} × {right, left (mirrored canvas)}. The flash frame is painted with the same code but every fill becomes #ffe94a and shade dots #ff7a1a, dots/details are skipped.
  6. Per frame: ground chunks (cached) → ground overlay (appear bursts, main-character glow) → collectibles → shadows → y-sorted props/creatures/main character → orbiting objects → projectiles → particles → numbers → screen overlay (vignette dots + panel frame). Everything except the ground is drawImage of baked canvases with scale/rotation.
  7. Sprites are drawn with smoothing on (the style is anti-aliased vector print, not pixel art).

World and ground

The floor is computed per pixel in world space so dots never swim or seam between chunks (256-unit chunks, at most 2.5 px/unit). It is warm newsprint split into large organic regions — meadow (base), autumn field, night, graveyard — crossed by winding paths:

  • Region fields: four domain-warped fBm fields (warp: Perlin at 1/900 × 110 units; field scales 1/1000, 1/1250, 1/820) minus thresholds (0.55, 0.635, 0.625) decide autumn, night and graveyard regions; a fourth field (0.02 − |perlin(x/1500)|) × 3.2 makes winding paths. The topmost positive layer wins (path > graveyard > night > autumn > meadow).
  • Grid + signed distance: fields are sampled on a 4-unit grid and bilinearly interpolated; the bilinear gradient gives a signed distance field / |∇field|. Cells far inside/outside are flagged so most pixels skip the work.
  • Ink contours: ink coverage (hw − |dist|) × res + 0.5, clamped 0..1, where half-width hw = 0.42 + 0.62 × noise(x/36) — a 0.84–2.08-unit brush line that thickens and thins. Ground ink is rgb 30,24,36.
  • Dot screens (one per material, multiply-blended into the paper colour):

Dot radius is interpolated by a tone field 0.5 + perlin(x/240)·0.42 + perlin(x/1300)·0.3, so the print gets denser and lighter in patches like a worn screen. Coverage is anti-aliased: (radius − d) × res + 0.5.

  • Path hatching: within 14 units of a path edge, diagonal lines (x − y)·0.7071 / 1.7 of half-width 0.34 × taper that fade over a noise-varying length 1.5–10.5 units — the comic "speed hatching" along a road.
  • Paper: × yellowing 1 ± 0.04 (noise/1700), × grain 0.985 + (hash − 0.5)·0.045 + fibre·0.025.
  • Decals (scatter every ~15 units, skipped within 5 units of a region edge): meadow — 3-blade ink grass tufts (30 %), 5-petal white/pink (#ff9ac8) flowers with a yellow centre and 0.35-unit ink outline (6 %); autumn — red/yellow leaves, stubble strokes; night — white 4-point glints; graveyard — branching cracks, pebbles; path — pebbles with a black crescent, ink specks; 1.5 % rare ink splats anywhere.
  • Layer order bottom to top: paper tint × yellowing → rotated dot screen → grain → ink contours + path hatching → ink decals. Contact shadows, glows and props sit on top (see their sections).

Characters

All figures use the same part painter so they share outline weight (~1 unit, doubled offset stroke), dot spacing (1.3 units) and the top-left light. Each figure is 10–20 primitive parts (ellipses, capsules, polygons, superellipses, bezier blobs) painted back-to-front, gets a shade crescent + spot-black crescent per part, a cyan plate behind it (magenta for ghosts) and four walk frames plus a yellow flash copy, baked facing right and mirrored for left. Readability in crowds comes from: saturated colours vs. pale ground, distinct silhouettes per type, big yellow eyes, the cyan fringe separating overlapping sprites, and the halftone shadow anchoring each one.

Main character — a figure with a round orange pumpkin head and a carved glowing face, blue cape and red scarf, ~21 × 34 units at 1.12× scale, feet at y = 0:

  • Head: superellipse pumpkin (|cos|^0.85, |sin|^0.92, dented 15 % at the top, 7 % at the bottom) centred at (0.4, −22.6), rx 8.4, ry 6.9, orange #ff8a1c, outline 1.15, shade crescent [-2.3, -1.9] with #e0301e dots rf 0.55, spot [-0.85, -0.75], four curved rib lines #d8561a 0.55 wide, two white shine ellipses top-left. Green stem capsule #4c8a2a (r 1.05). Face = carved triangles and a zig-zag grin: black hole, yellow #ffe23a interior shifted (+0.3, +0.55) inside, leaving a black rim on the top-left.
  • Body: blue cape quad-curve shape (shade [1.7, -1.3], spot [0.75, -0.55], two fold lines 0.5), red scarf knot ellipse and a waving scarf tail polygon behind everything.
  • Limbs: capsules (CAP) — legs r 1.35 in #2b2846 (back leg darker #1f1c36), black ellipse shoes with a white shine; arms r 1.25 in cape blue with Ben-Day overlay, white glove ellipses #fffaf0 with cyan-dot shade.
  • Paint order: scarf tail → back leg → back arm → front leg → cape → front arm → scarf knot → stem → head → face.
  • Frames: 0 idle, 1 contact A, 2 passing (front foot lifted 1.9), 3 contact B; each frame is a row of numbers (back foot x/y, front foot x/y, back/front arm angle, cape flare). Cape flare also swings the hem and scarf tail.
  • Facing: bake once facing right, mirror the canvas for left (the face is offset +1.4 units toward the facing).
  • Plate: cyan behind the main character.

Small creature — a two-tone root creature with a leafy crown and an angry face, ~17 × 28 units at 0.92×: teardrop root body (bezier, top −20.6, bottom −2.6, half-width 8.5, outline 1.1, shade [-2.3, -1.5] rf 0.6, spot [-0.95, -0.6]), two-tone: magenta #d4308c or violet #8a3cc0 top with complementary dots (rf 0.42) above a wavy ink line (0.45 wide) at y −12.2 (amplitude 0.9), cream bottom with sparse orange dots (rf 0.22, rotated 30°). Three dotted leaves on top, stubby capsule legs (r 1.05). Face: yellow eye ellipses with ink pupils, angular ink brows, ink frown with a pink #ff5a6a tongue and white fangs. Two colour variants chosen by seed. Cyan plate.

Fast creature — a floating ghost, ~25 × 26 units: white sheet #fcfbf4 with a 7-unit dome and a wavy tail whose control points move with frame phase; strong cyan-dot shade [-2.6, -1.6] rf 0.62; cyan dots over the bottom 12 units; the bottom 28 % is erased with a destination-out gradient to 75 % so it hovers. Ink oval eyes with cyan catchlights, gaping ink mouth. Magenta plate (not cyan) so it reads as a different "ink".

Large creature — a giant root colossus, ~48 × 66 units: half-width 17.8, outline 1.45, plum #8a3a86 top over clay #c98545 bottom (shade #3a1030), five dark leaves, capsule arms r 3.0 in #9a5a30 with blob fists, zig-zag crack lines, a yellow star-shaped glowing core in the chest with ink rays, angular glowing eyes and a mouth with yellow teeth. Moves slower and uses a smaller flash cap (0.6).

Props

Scenery objects are baked with the same part painter as the characters (fill → dots → shade crescent → spot black → double outline, light top-left), but get a weaker pink #ff3d6e plate at 45 % instead of cyan at 65 %, so the characters pop in front of them. Each prop is baked in one or more variants and mirrored at random; each casts a halftone contact shadow scaled to its footprint (half-widths in units below).

  • Leafy tree (box 46 × 60, shadow 15): bark trunk #8a5634 polygon with a forked top (shade #3a2010 dots in a side crescent [1.4, 0], two thin bark ink lines 0.4), crown = union of 5–6 circles (radius 6–12.8) converted to one polygon (ray-cast outline, 144 samples) so the even-odd crescent works. Green crown #56b04a with #1f6e30 dots, or autumn crown #f07a2a with red #e8262c dots; outline 1.2, shade [-3.4, -3.6] rf 0.55, faint dot overlay rf 0.18, a white shine top-left and seven little hatched leaf arcs (0.5 ink). Trees sway by a horizontal skew of ±0.02 at 1.2 rad/s.
  • Dead tree (shadow 10): bare trunk and six capsule branches (r 0.65–1.2) in #6a4a52 with #2a1420 dot shade in a side crescent [0.9, 0], an ink knot-hole ellipse.
  • Berry bush (shadow 11): five-circle union #56b04a, shade #1f6e30, five red #e8262c berries (r 0.9) with ink rims.
  • Rocks (two sizes, shadow 9): bezier blob in stone #bdb8cc with #5a5272 dot shade [-2, -1.6], a white shine and one zig-zag ink crack.
  • Wooden fence (34 units wide, shadow 17): two rails and three pointed posts in wood #d39a58, shade dots #7a4520.
  • Signpost (shadow 5): wooden pole, cream board #fbf1d0 with a red arrow and two ink nail dots.
  • Pumpkins (shadow 11): a pair of uncarved pumpkins (rx 8.2 and 5.2) or one lit pumpkin (rx 8.8) with a carved glowing face — the same superellipse, ribs #d8561a and stem as the main character's head.

Effects and particles

All effects are baked sprites from the same painter (ink outline, dot fills, plates) — never blurred.

  • Impact burst (0.16 s): 8-point yellow star r 6.5/2.4 (outline 0.75) with red dots and a white inner star, scale 0.5 → 1.2 → 0.3; 45 % chance of 7 black tapered speed-line wedges radiating from r 4.5 outward. The struck figure is overdrawn with its yellow flash frame.
  • Big burst (0.34 s; large creature 0.5 s at ×1.9): 12-point jagged star r 10/5.6 (jitter 0.38, outline 0.9) in yellow/white/orange/yellow with red, cyan, dark-red or purple dot rings (rf 0.62 out to r 6.2, rf 0.95 inside r 3.6); pops with outBack in the first 16 %, grows +35 %/life, fades the last 40 %.
  • Chunks: 4 (small creature), 2 (ghost), 9 (large creature) five-sided polygons in the creature's colours with spot-black crescent, thrown with gravity 280; plus 2–6 ink droplets (ghosts: cyan).
  • Sound words: POW! ZACK! KRACH! PENG! ZONK! PLOPP! for normal bursts (30 % chance, ≥ 1.3 s apart), bigger WUMMS!/RUMMS! for large-creature bursts (always) with a 16-wedge ring shockwave, AUTSCH! when the main character is struck (≥ 2.2 s apart). Max 3 alive. Balloon: Bangers 11 units centred in a 13-point jagged star (inner 0.72), a darker offset plate (−0.8, +0.7, 70 %), a dot rim outside 62 % of the radius, 1.0 ink outline. Colour sets [fill, text, plate]: yellow/red/orange-dot, red/yellow/dark-red, cyan/white/dark-blue, white/red/cyan.
  • Pop-up numbers: Bangers 9.5 units white (13 units yellow when emphasised, inside a white 11-point star balloon); see Typography. Scale 0.3 → 1.35 → 1.0 over the first 24 % of life, random tilt ±0.15 rad.
  • Projectile: cream seed sprite #fff1c8 with orange halftone shade and a magenta plate, spinning via y-squash (0.6–1.0), trailed by three black triangular streaks (15/10/11 units long, 85 % alpha).
  • Collectibles: small = cyan teardrop gem with #0a5a9a dot shade; large = magenta 5-point star with yellow dots and a yellow centre disc; both bob ±1 unit above a tiny dot shadow and carry a magenta plate.
  • Orbiting object: a small purple #9a3cc0 root-shaped lantern with a leaf crown and a carved glowing face, bobbing ±1.4 units, rocking ±0.14 rad, over a yellow dot glow at 0.8 scale with flickering alpha 0.75 ± 0.15.
  • Motion smear (fast move): cyan and magenta silhouettes at 6 and 12 units behind, 60 % alpha, plus 4 ink speed lines 16–24 units long; 3 white puffs.
  • Footstep / launch puffs: white circle-union cloud with cyan-dot shade, outBack pop, quadratic shrink.
  • Celebration ring: yellow ring of 16 wedges + 8 white 4-point twinkles with cyan shade flying out.
  • Appear burst: flattened (y × 0.45) yellow 12-point star with red dots and a black hole on the ground; the creature grows with outElastic; ink droplets fly out.

Atmosphere and lighting

  • Light is always top-left; it exists only as crescent placement and white shine ellipses. No light sources are rendered as gradients.
  • Contact shadows are two concentric ellipses of ink dots (outer 9.6 × 3.5 units, rf 0.5, spacing 1.15; inner 6.4 × 2.3, rf 0.95), drawn at 55 % alpha (main character 65 %, ghost 35 %) and scaled per type (large creature 19, small creature 8.4, ghost 6; props use their footprint, e.g. tree 15, fence 17).
  • Dot glow: a hex grid of yellow #ffe23a dots (spacing 1.6) within radius 15, dot radius (1 − d)^0.7 × 0.88, squashed to 1.5 × 0.62 on the floor at 80 % alpha under the main character. Orbiting lanterns get the same glow at 0.8 scale with flickering alpha.
  • Plates as atmosphere: the misregistered cyan/magenta/pink fringe behind every sprite is the main "print" signature — keep it on everything.
  • No blur, no bloom, no colour grading.
  • Screen overlay (baked once per resolution): a hex grid of ink dots (spacing max(7, H/100) px) whose radius grows with smoothstep(0.9, 1.42, distance from centre) at 42 % alpha — a halftone vignette; a paper gutter of max(4, 0.6 % H) px; and a black panel frame of the same width drawn as jittered 24-segment strokes (width ×0.9–1.2) so it looks hand-inked. Only tiles that contain pixels are blitted.

UI

Comic panels: zero radius, 3 px ink borders, solid offset shadows (4–8 px, no blur), slight rotations (±1–4°), skewed bars, dotted fills via radial-gradient patterns. The counter is a white oval speech bubble with a tail; the big title sits in a 16-point red starburst with yellow dots; cards are cream with dotted colour headers (cyan, yellow, pink), rotated −1.5°/1°/−0.5° and popping in with an overshoot cubic-bezier. Icons are painted with the same part painter on a 48-unit grid (outline 2.4, dot spacing 3.6) with a 1.4 px misregistered plate.

Motion and animation

  • Main character walk: frame sequence 1, 2, 3, 2 at anim × 0.4; lift sin(f·π) × 1.8 units, tilt 0.05 × facing × sin(f·π); a dust puff on every other step (50 %).
  • Main character idle: sy = 1 + 0.025 sin(3.2t), sx = 1 − 0.018 sin(3.2t). Launching a projectile squashes 5 %; the celebration wobble is sin(t·4π) ±16 %; when struck it overlays the yellow flash and squashes 14/12 %, then blinks at 16 Hz down to 45 % alpha.
  • Small creature: 4 frames at anim × 0.85, rotation sin × 0.09, hop 1.2 units, 5 % squash at contact, random scale 0.94–1.06.
  • Ghost: 4 tail frames at 7 fps, hover 4.5 units up, bob ±1.6 at 2.6 rad/s, lean vx/64 × 0.14, alpha 0.94.
  • Large creature: 4 frames at anim × 0.32, contact squash (1 − up)^3 × 7 %, rotation ±0.03, hop 2 units.
  • Appear: solid creatures grow vertically with outElastic (width compensates 1/√p), ghosts fade + outBack.
  • Struck: flash frame alpha min(cap, flash × 1.4), squash sx +18 %, sy −14 %.
  • Vanish: 0–0.2 s inflate to 1.25× in the yellow frame (outQuad), 0.2–0.42 s shrink to 0 (inQuad) while rotating +0.6 rad; the big burst covers it.
  • Trees sway with a skew of ±0.02 at 1.2 rad/s; HUD shake = 0.22 s steps(3) jitter of ±4 px and ±1.5°.
  • Pops everywhere use overshoot (outBack, outElastic, CSS cubic-bezier(.3,1.8,.5,1)).

Do and don't

Do:

  • Route every shape through one part painter (fill → dots → shade crescent → spot black → double outline).
  • Bake sprites once: 4 frames × normal/flash × both facings; blit at runtime.
  • Shade with dot size in the colour's darker sister; keep the light top-left.
  • Slip a cyan/magenta plate 0.5 units behind every sprite (pink at 45 % for props).
  • Keep characters saturated and the ground pale.
  • Use jagged star balloons + Bangers for loud moments, rate-limited to 3 on screen.
  • Give every UI panel a hard offset ink shadow and zero radius.

Don't:

  • Use gradients, shadowBlur, filters or soft glows.
  • Use pure #000 or hairline outlines (< 0.4 units).
  • Give two ground regions the same dot angle and spacing.
  • Spam sound words — more than 3 turns into noise.
  • Let creatures share the dominant colour of the ground region they walk on.
  • Round UI corners or use translucent glass panels.
  • Compute halftone per frame for sprites.

Implementation recipe

  1. Set up a camera that shows 360 world units vertically; compute S = screenPx / 360 and K = clamp(round(S × 1.4), 3, 5).
  2. Copy the palette; for every new colour define a darker dot sister.
  3. Implement dotPattern(colour, rf, T) (cached diamond-lattice tile) and the Painter with dots, crescent, part, detail (see Code / starter.js).
  4. Implement shape helpers: ellipse E, capsule CAP, polygon POLY, bezier blob, circle union → polygon, star points with seeded jitter, plus any signature shapes (pumpkin superellipse, teardrop root body).
  5. Model each figure as a function parts(P, frame) painting back-to-front. Store walk poses as small number tables.
  6. build(box, fn, mode, plate) each frame into a canvas at K px/unit with 1.9 units margin, then add the plate silhouette destination-over at 65 % alpha offset (−0.5, +0.375) units. Build mode = 'flash' too. Mirror for left-facing.
  7. Bake shadows (two dot ellipses), the dot glow (hex dot grid), bursts, impact star, chunks, puffs, twinkles, props (pink plate at 45 %).
  8. Ground: noise fields on a 4-unit grid → bilinear signed distance → per-pixel region, ink contour of varying half-width, one rotated AA dot screen per region, grain, path hatching; render in chunks (generator/worker); then scatter ink decals.
  9. Per frame: ground → appear bursts + glow → collectibles → shadows → y-sorted figures/props → projectiles → particles → numbers → baked screen overlay.
  10. Hook visual events: impact → impact burst + flash; vanish → big burst + chunks + ink + maybe a sound word; fast move → plate smear.
  11. Build the HUD in HTML/CSS with the panel rules above, fonts Bangers + Comic Neue 700.
  12. Check readability with 200 creatures: if they mush together, raise saturation contrast to the floor, not outline width.

Code

The core techniques, standalone. A complete runnable kit (main character, small creature, ground, overlay, bursts, sound words) is in starter.js; the full reference module is ../../js/styles/popart.js.

1. Halftone tile + part painter (the heart of the style):

const INK = '#1b1620', TAU = Math.PI * 2;
const PAT = new Map();
function dotPattern(ctx, col, rf, T) {           // T px tile, dots at corners + centre (45° lattice)
  const key = col + '|' + rf + '|' + T;
  if (PAT.has(key)) return PAT.get(key);
  const cv = document.createElement('canvas'); cv.width = cv.height = T;
  const c = cv.getContext('2d'), r = rf * T * 0.5;
  c.fillStyle = col; c.beginPath();
  for (const q of [[0, 0], [T, 0], [0, T], [T, T], [T / 2, T / 2]]) { c.moveTo(q[0] + r, q[1]); c.arc(q[0], q[1], r, 0, TAU); }
  c.fill();
  const p = ctx.createPattern(cv, 'repeat'); PAT.set(key, p); return p;
}
function Painter(c, k) { this.c = c; this.k = k; this.ol = 1.0; this.dsp = 1.3; }
Painter.prototype.dots = function (col, rf, sp, rot) {
  const T = Math.max(3, Math.round((sp || this.dsp) * this.k)), p = dotPattern(this.c, col, rf, T);
  let m = new DOMMatrix(); if (rot) m = m.rotate(rot);
  p.setTransform(m.scale(1 / this.k));            // dots measured in world units
  this.c.fillStyle = p; this.c.fillRect(-999, -999, 1999, 1999);
};
Painter.prototype.crescent = function (shape, sh, fn) { // area of shape NOT covered by shape shifted by sh
  const c = this.c; c.save(); c.beginPath(); c.rect(-999, -999, 1999, 1999);
  c.translate(sh[0], sh[1]); shape(c); c.translate(-sh[0], -sh[1]); c.clip('evenodd'); fn(); c.restore();
};
Painter.prototype.part = function (shape, fill, o = {}) {
  const c = this.c;
  c.save(); c.beginPath(); shape(c); c.clip();
  c.fillStyle = fill; c.fillRect(-999, -999, 1999, 1999);
  if (o.dots) this.dots(...o.dots);
  if (o.sh) this.crescent(shape, o.sh, () => this.dots(...(o.shade || [INK, 0.45])));
  if (o.spot) this.crescent(shape, o.spot, () => { c.fillStyle = INK; c.fillRect(-999, -999, 1999, 1999); });
  if (o.hi) { c.fillStyle = '#fff'; for (const h of o.hi) { c.beginPath(); c.ellipse(h[0], h[1], h[2], h[3], h[4] || 0, 0, TAU); c.fill(); } }
  c.restore();
  const w = o.ol != null ? o.ol : this.ol;      // brush outline: heavier bottom-right
  c.strokeStyle = INK; c.lineWidth = w; c.beginPath(); shape(c); c.stroke();
  c.save(); c.translate(w * 0.3, w * 0.36); c.lineWidth = w * 0.85; c.beginPath(); shape(c); c.stroke(); c.restore();
};
// usage (world units, light top-left) – the main character's pumpkin head:
// P.part(E(0,-22.6,8.4,6.9), '#ff8a1c', { ol: 1.15, sh: [-2.3,-1.9], shade: ['#e0301e', 0.55], spot: [-0.85,-0.75], hi: [[-4,-26.2,2.1,0.85,-0.6]] });

2. Bake with a misregistered colour plate:

function bakeSprite(box, paint, K, plate) {      // box = [l, t, r, b] in world units, feet at y = 0
  const m = 1.9, l = box[0] - m, t = box[1] - m;
  const cv = document.createElement('canvas');
  cv.width = Math.ceil((box[2] + m - l) * K); cv.height = Math.ceil((box[3] + m - t) * K);
  const ctx = cv.getContext('2d');
  ctx.scale(K, K); ctx.translate(-l, -t); ctx.lineJoin = ctx.lineCap = 'round';
  paint(new Painter(ctx, K));
  if (plate) {
    const tn = document.createElement('canvas'); tn.width = cv.width; tn.height = cv.height;
    const tc = tn.getContext('2d'); tc.drawImage(cv, 0, 0);
    tc.globalCompositeOperation = 'source-atop'; tc.fillStyle = plate; tc.fillRect(0, 0, tn.width, tn.height);
    ctx.setTransform(1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0);
    ctx.globalCompositeOperation = 'destination-over'; ctx.globalAlpha = 0.65;
    ctx.drawImage(tn, -0.5 * K, 0.375 * K);       // plate slips 0.5 units left, 0.375 down
  }
  return { c: cv, ox: -l * K, oy: -t * K };       // draw at (x - ox/K, y - oy/K), size / K
}

3. Ground pixel: rotated anti-aliased dot screen + brush contour:

// x, y world units; res = px per unit; tone 0..1; dist = signed distance to region edge (units); lw = noise 0..1
function groundPixel(x, y, res, paper, scr, tone, dist, lw) {
  let r = paper[0], g = paper[1], b = paper[2];
  if (scr) {
    const ca = Math.cos(scr.a), sa = Math.sin(scr.a);
    const su = (x * ca + y * sa) / scr.sp, sv = (y * ca - x * sa) / scr.sp;
    const fu = su - Math.floor(su) - 0.5, fv = sv - Math.floor(sv) - 0.5;
    const d = Math.hypot(fu, fv) * scr.sp, rad = scr.sp * (scr.r0 + (scr.r1 - scr.r0) * tone);
    const cov = Math.min(1, (rad - d) * res + 0.5);                 // 1-pixel AA edge
    if (cov > 0) { r *= 1 - cov * (1 - scr.ink[0] / 255); g *= 1 - cov * (1 - scr.ink[1] / 255); b *= 1 - cov * (1 - scr.ink[2] / 255); }
  }
  const hw = 0.42 + 0.62 * lw;                                      // contour half-width 0.42..1.04
  const ink = Math.max(0, Math.min(1, (hw - Math.abs(dist)) * res + 0.5));
  return [r + (30 - r) * ink, g + (24 - g) * ink, b + (36 - b) * ink];
}
// meadow: groundPixel(x, y, 2.5, [237,233,199], { ink: [104,176,98], sp: 3.3, a: 0.785, r0: 0.17, r1: 0.33 }, tone, dist, lw)

4. Halftone contact shadow and dot glow (no blur anywhere):

function bakeShadow(P) {                          // paint into a Painter-backed canvas, box [-10,-4,10,4]
  const c = P.c;
  c.save(); c.beginPath(); c.ellipse(0, 0, 9.6, 3.5, 0, 0, TAU); c.clip(); P.dots(INK, 0.5, 1.15); c.restore();
  c.save(); c.beginPath(); c.ellipse(0, 0, 6.4, 2.3, 0, 0, TAU); c.clip(); P.dots(INK, 0.95, 1.15); c.restore();
}
function bakeGlow(c) {                            // box [-16,-16,16,16]; draw at 1.5 x 0.62 under the main character, alpha 0.8
  const sp = 1.6; c.fillStyle = '#ffe23a'; c.beginPath();
  for (let j = -10; j <= 10; j++) for (let i = -10; i <= 10; i++) {
    const x = i * sp + (j & 1 ? sp / 2 : 0), y = j * sp * 0.866, d = Math.hypot(x, y) / 15;
    if (d >= 1) continue;
    const r = Math.pow(1 - d, 0.7) * sp * 0.55; c.moveTo(x + r, y); c.arc(x, y, r, 0, TAU);
  }
  c.fill();
}

Adapting it to your project

Keep fixed: the painter pipeline and its order, ink #1b1620, outline ~1 unit with the doubled offset stroke, top-left light, dot spacing ~1.3 units on sprites and ~3 units on the ground, misregistered cyan/magenta plates, no blur, comic bursts + Bangers, hard-shadow UI.

May change:

  • Palette: swap process colours freely, but each new fill needs a darker dot sister and must stay more saturated than the floor. Keep plates to two colours (cyan + magenta or a pair from your palette).
  • Theme: sci-fi → chrome blue robots with cyan-dot shade and a magenta plate; western → leather browns with red dot overlays. The ground regions become desert/rock/water with their own screen angles.
  • New figures: build from ellipses/capsules/polygons, give each type its own silhouette and dominant colour, carved or ink faces with big yellow eyes. Large figures: thicker outline (1.4–1.6) and a glowing core detail.
  • Mixing elements: the characters work over any light, low-saturation world; the world works under any saturated, outlined figures. Keep the dot-shadow and the plate fringe so mixed elements still read as print.
  • Camera / layout: side views work well (keep the shade on the bottom-right); for side-on scenes, turn region fields into horizontal bands and keep the contour lines.
  • Resolution: everything is in world units; only K changes. At very low resolutions raise dot spacing so dots stay ≥ 3 px.
  • Sound words: localise the word lists; keep them short, uppercase, with "!".

Performance notes

  • Bake cost: ~40 figure canvases (4 frames × 2 modes × 2 facings per type) plus props and effects at 3–5 px/unit — a few hundred ms once at start. Runtime per figure is one drawImage (+1 for the flash), so 300+ creatures are fine.
  • The ground is the hot spot: up to 4 region distances, a rotated dot screen, grain and hatching per pixel. Mitigations used by the reference module: 4-unit noise grid with bilinear interpolation, per-cell "deep inside / far outside" flags that skip distance work, 256-unit chunks rendered as a generator yielding every 2 rows, resolution capped at 2.5 px/unit, putImageData in 128-row strips.
  • Text sprites (numbers, sound words) are cached by value and colour; clear the cache above ~600 entries. Rebuild them once the web font has loaded (check document.fonts every 0.5 s).
  • The screen overlay is baked once per resolution and only non-empty tiles are redrawn.
  • starter.js bakes the ground once per canvas size and then draws a frame in well under 1 ms.
