Bouncy Cartoon — Game Art Style Spec

Candy-bright HD vector cartoon: thick deep-plum outlines, one cel-shade crescent, one white gloss and bouncy squash & stretch.

A clean, high-resolution vector cartoon. Every object is a chunky, rounded silhouette with one thick deep-plum outline (#3a2240), a flat fill, exactly one darker cel-shade crescent and one white glossy highlight. Characters are big-headed, round and expressive (angry brows, googly eyes, carved grins) and bounce constantly with squash and stretch. The ground is a flat pastel play-mat of soft grass, lilac and sand regions with crisp edges and tiny vector tufts and flowers, so the outlined figures pop clearly even when several hundred of them crowd the screen. The examples below are a pumpkin-headed main character, small purple-and-cream root-bulb creatures, floating white ghosts and a large root giant.

When to use it

  • Great fit: scenes with hundreds of on-screen figures that must stay readable – the single outline colour merges each figure into one crisp sticker-like silhouette.
  • Great fit: casual, family-friendly or party games; arena shooters; shop or inventory screens with lots of item icons (icons use the very same painter at 2.6 px outline on a 48 px grid).
  • Great fit for AI-generated content: shapes are ellipses, capsules and blobs described by a handful of numbers – new characters and props are fast to author.
  • Bad fit: horror, grim drama, realistic sims – the style is relentlessly cheerful.
  • Bad fit: atmospheric exploration that relies on darkness or lighting; there is no lighting system, only implied top-left light.

Palette rules

  • Every colour is a pair: fill + one darker shade, about 15–25% darker and a touch more saturated, often shifted toward purple/warm (#ff952b → #ea681c, #f6f8ff → #c3d0ef). Never a third tone on a body.
  • The outline is always #3a2240. Never black, never per-object outline colours.
  • Ground colours are pastel and desaturated; characters are saturated. The main character is warm (orange + teal), creatures are cool (purple, white) – the main character never gets lost in a crowd.
  • Highlights are pure white at alpha 0.92. Ground shadows are plum at 20%, never grey.
  • #ffd43f is reserved for light, stars, collect sparkles and highlighted numbers.

Rendering technique

Shared by every element (characters, props, effects, icons):

  1. Choose sprite resolution. K = clamp(round(screenPxPerUnit * 1.25), 3, 6) canvas px per world unit, where screenPxPerUnit = displayHeight / 360.
  2. Describe every sprite as a part list fn(P) declared back-to-front: P.part(shapeFn, fill, { shade, sh:[dx,dy], inner, detail, hi, hi2, ol }) plus P.detail(fn) for faces and strokes.
  3. Bake each sprite in two passes into its own canvas (ctx.scale(K,K), round joins/caps):
    • Outline pass: every part is stroked and filled in #3a2240 with lineWidth = 2 * 1.15 u. Because all parts are filled plum, overlaps merge into one silhouette with one even 1.15 u border.
    • Fill pass: each part clips to its shape, optionally strokes a 0.55 u inner separation line (lineWidth 1.1, only the inner half survives the clip), fills the whole part with the shade colour, then clips again to the same shape translated by sh (e.g. [-1.9, -1.6]) and fills the lit colour. The uncovered sliver bottom-right is the cel crescent. Then detail, then gloss: white ellipse hi at alpha 0.92 plus a white dot hi2.
    • Flash pass (third copy): same, but lit = #ffffff, shade = #efe6f6, no details.
  4. Bake 4 animation frames for each animated figure, a flash copy of each, and mirrored copies (or mirror at blit time with scale(-1,1)).
  5. Per frame: ground chunks (cached images) → ground overlay (dirt holes) → gem collectibles → one batched shadow path → y-sorted props, creatures, main character → orbiting objects → projectiles → particles → floating numbers → world overlay (butterflies) → HTML HUD.
  6. Blit with transforms only: translate(x, y) · rotate(tilt) · scale(sx*facing, sy) around the feet, then drawImage(c, -ox/K, -oy/K, w/K, h/K). All squash/stretch is done here.
  7. Everything is drawn in world units (360 units visible vertically); no pixel snapping needed.

World and ground

The ground is a flat-colour play-mat with no outlines – it stays visually behind every outlined figure and prop.

  • Base #9ec671 (meadow).
  • Regions are 2-octave fbm noise fields with a gentle domain warp (perlin at 1/900, ±90 u), each thresholded so value > 0 means inside: dark grass #79ae5a (fbm(X/520) - 0.6), lilac meadows #c8b6da (fbm(X/800) - 0.635), clay fields #daa070 (fbm(X/1150) - 0.615), sand clearings #ebd3a0 (fbm(X/700) - 0.66) and sand paths of even width = (0.0175 - |perlin(x/1700)|) * 3 (the zero contour of a noise field, so it winds and branches). Layers stack bottom → top in that order. Regions are big and calm – 100–400 units across.
  • Crisp edges with a rim: fields are sampled on a coarse 4-unit grid. Per pixel, the value s and its gradient are bilinearly interpolated; dist = s / |grad| (in pixels) is an analytic signed distance. Coverage clamp(dist + 0.5, 0, 1) gives a 1-px anti-aliased edge; a rim factor clamp(rimPx - dist + 0.5, 0, 1) blends in the rim colour for a darker band just inside the edge: dark grass rim #6a9e50 1.7 u, lilac #b4a0cc 2.0 u, clay #c78a60 2.3 u, sand #d8ba85 2.5 u. Grid cells fully inside/outside are flat-filled (fast path).
  • Brightness tint ±3.5% from noise at 1/1500.
  • Decals on a 12-unit jittered grid, only where the material is the same within 4.5 u. They are flat (no outline, no crescent):
    • meadow: 30% grass tufts (3 curved blades, stroke 0.8 u, rgb(130,175,94)), 6% flowers (5 petals radius 0.72·s at distance 0.95·s, white/cream/yellow #f8de6c/pink #f4b3c8, centre #f2a23a), 4% dot clusters;
    • dark grass: 38% tufts rgb(98,150,74); lilac: 50% flowers (#f3e9fb, #a98bd4, #f6bad3, centre #f6d36a) + 12% tufts rgb(170,150,192);
    • clay: fallen leaves (#c9773f, #e3ad5c, #bf5f3e) with a 0.35 u vein;
    • sand: 10% pebbles (3 stacked ellipses #b9a588, #cdbfae, #ddd2c4), 16% dots.
  • Dirt holes (ground decal from which ground creatures emerge): a dirt ellipse #b07a4c with a 0.8 u plum rim and a dark centre #43283f grows with outBack; dirt crumbs (circles r 1.3 #9c6a42, plum 0.5 u rim) fly out.
  • Chunks are 256 units, rendered at up to 3 px/unit, generated incrementally (generator with yield every few rows).

Characters

All figures use the shared two-pass painter: one plum outline (1.15 u outer, 0.55 u inner lines), one cel crescent at the lower-right, one white gloss at the upper-left, 4 baked frames plus a white flash copy. Heads are big (~60% of the height), bodies and limbs tiny and stubby, and every figure type has its own unmistakable silhouette.

Main character (pumpkin-head kid) – box [-15,-34] .. [15,2.5], drawn at scale 1.1 (≈ 25 u wide, 37 u tall incl. stem), feet at y = 0.

  • Head: squircle pumpkin path (|cos|^0.85 * rx, |sin|^0.92 * ry, gaussian dents at top and bottom) centred at (0.4, -19.6), rx 11.2, ry 9.1. Fill #ff952b, shade #ea681c, crescent offset [-1.9, -1.6], inner line 0.55, four rib curves (quadraticCurveTo, 0.6 u, #ec7a22). Gloss ellipse at (cx-6.3, cy-4.3) radii 2.5×1.3 rot -0.6 and a 0.6 u dot.
  • Face (carved): offset +2.2 u toward the facing side. Triangle eyes (rounded 0.8) and a crescent grin: fill the hole #8a3214, clip, translate (0.25, 0.85) and fill the same shape #ffd43f → visible carve depth on top. Hot cores #fff3b0 in the eyes, 3 rounded pumpkin teeth clipped into the mouth. Pained-face variant: chevron > < eyes and an O mouth.
  • Stem capsule (0.1,-27)→(1.2,-30.9) r 1.3 #7f9b3b; one leaf (len 5.4, width 2) with midrib; a 0.6 u plum curly tendril.
  • Body: teal #2f9c95 ellipse 4.5×4.4 at (0,-8.3) with two cream buttons; green scarf ellipse 5.7×2.3 at y -11 plus a blob scarf tail hanging back with two light stripes #c3ec8c and fringe strokes.
  • Limbs: capsules r 1.15 (arms, length 3.4) with cream ball hands r 1.5; legs #41703f r 1.35 from y -5.6 to the feet, shoes #6b3d2c = ellipse 2.1×1.35 with a tiny gloss.
  • 4 frames [backFootX, backFootY, frontFootX, frontFootY, backArm, frontArm, scarfPhase]: [-2.3,0, 2.3,0, .25,-.25, 0], [-3.7,-.2, 3.9,-.5, .9,-.8, 1.6], [-2,-1.5, 2.9,-2.1, .35,-.15, 3.2], [2.7,-.6, -1.3,-.2, -.75,.85, 4.8].
  • Facing = horizontal mirror of the baked canvas.

Small creature (grumpy root bulb) – scale 0.9, box [-14,-32]..[14,2.5].

  • Bulb body (bezier onion shape, top -21.6, bottom -2.6, half-width 10.4) with a two-tone fill: cream #f8eacb bottom, purple #9b5cd0 top separated by a sine wave (y = -10.8 + 0.8 sin(0.9x + 0.6)), both with their own shades (#e0c595, #7241aa). Crescent [-1.8,-1.5].
  • Three leaves (lengths 8.2, 8, 10.2) on top, swaying ±0.1 rad per frame. Two beige root capsule legs r 1.45.
  • Face: googly eyes (white ellipses 2.5×2.8 and 2.3×2.7 with 0.7 u plum rim, pupils r 1.25 looking toward the front, white catchlights r 0.38), angry brows 1.35 u, small frown arc 0.9 u.
  • Three colour variants for the top: purple #9b5cd0, magenta #bd5aa6, indigo #7d66d8.

Fast creature (floating ghost) – box [-15,-28]..[14,2.5].

  • Body: #f6f8ff bezier dome (shade #c3d0ef) with a 3-scallop hem animated by phase and a wispy tail curling back; little stub arms as ellipses.
  • Eyes: plum ovals with #9fd8ff catchlights; gaping plum mouth with pink tongue #ff7f9e and two white fangs.
  • After baking, the bottom 9 u fade to 42% alpha (destination-in linear gradient), so it looks like it hovers.

Large creature (root giant) – box [-28,-68]..[29,3], about 2.5× the small creature.

  • Body 39 u wide, 41 u tall, purple #7b3f99 top / ochre #efc96f bottom (shades #58297a / #cf9f4c), crescent [-2.8,-2.3].
  • Five big leaves in two greens, stubby root legs r 3.5 with ellipse feet, thick capsule arms r 3.1.
  • A bandage cross (#f6e6c4, rounded rects 11.5×3.4 at ±0.68 rad) and a bandaged forearm, zig-zag crack lines 0.75 u.
  • Glowing #ffd43f eyes, heavy plum brow wedges and a wide plum grimace with two rows of cream triangle teeth (#fff6e2).

Creating new figures: start from a distinctive silhouette (blob, capsule tower, bean, star), give it a big readable face (googly eyes + brows, or a carved glowing face), a fill/shade pair, 4 frames, a flash copy and its own shadow size. Large figures are 2–2.5× the small ones; a giant centrepiece can be 4×.

Props

Scenery is painted with exactly the same two-pass painter as the characters, so it carries the same plum outline, crescent and gloss. Each prop is baked once (1–5 variants, plus mirrored copies) and stands on its own flat plum shadow.

  • Round tree – box [-20,-64]..[20,2]. Tapered bark trunk #a56f48 / shade #7c4e33 with a small knot circle. Canopy = 6 overlapping circles (radius 7–11.5 u) merged as one part, crescent [-2.8,-2.6], six small 0.75 u shade arcs, gloss ellipse 3.3×1.7 plus a dot. Canopy pairs: green #7fca5c/#57a243, orange #f2a64c/#d57c36, lime #a9d65a/#7fb343, gold #efc54e/#d09d36. The green tree carries three red apples (r 1.4, #ef5a52, 0.5 u plum rim, white catchlight).
  • Tiered pine – three rounded triangles (corner radius 2.6 u, half-widths 14 / 11.5 / 9, heights 18 / 16 / 14) in #4fa564 / shade #36804b over a short bark trunk, gloss on the top tier.
  • Bush – box [-15,-20]..[15,2], 4 ellipses as one part, crescent [-2.1,-2.0], three shade arcs. Greens #73c257/#4e9a40, #8acb5a/#61a444, #9ad16a/#6daa4c. Variants: red berries (r 1.15 with plum rim and catchlight) or pink 5-petal flowers #f7b5d0 with #ffd43f centres.
  • Rock – 7-point Catmull-Rom blob #bdb5cd / shade #918aa9 with a small zig-zag crack line, in three sizes (×0.66, ×1, ×1.2); the large one wears a mossy cap #8cc65a / #66a443.
  • Pumpkin – squircle pumpkin #f59a3a / shade #d96f26 with rib lines, a short stem, a leaf with midrib and a curly tendril; single (rx 8.8, ry 6.6) or a pair with a smaller one (rx 6.0).
  • Mushrooms – a pair of different sizes: cream stalks #f6ead2 / #d9c6a4, caps red #ec5b52 / #c03f45 or brown #c98a52 / #a4683a with cream dots #fff6e6.
  • Fence – two rounded rails 35×2.7 u and three pointed posts with nail dots, wood #d4a066 / shade #a9743f.
  • Signpost – wooden post plus an arrow-shaped board #e4b77b / #c38f56 with a small painted pumpkin and an arrow.
  • Hay stack – domed bezier mound #f3cf62 / shade #d6a33c with 16 straw strokes #c99434, 6 light strokes #fbe9a4 and a few straws sticking out of the top.
  • Placement by region: meadow = round trees, bushes, rocks, mushrooms, fences, signs; dark grass = pines, berry bushes, mushrooms, mossy rocks; lilac = flower bushes, lime trees, small rocks; clay = pumpkins, orange/gold trees, hay, fences, brown mushrooms; sand = rocks, hay, signs, fences.
  • Sway: trees and bushes skew around their base (sin(1.3t + seed) × 0.025 for trees, 0.035 for bushes); everything else is static.

Effects and particles

All particles are baked sprites from the same painter (outlined, crescented):

  • Projectile: cream seed (#fff1c8, shade #e6c68a, outline 0.85 u) whose height scales 0.55 + 0.45|cos(spin)| to fake spin, with a white speed streak: three round-cap lines (1.0 u centre, 0.75 u sides at ±2 u) with a linear gradient alpha 0.95 → 0 over 14 units behind it. Small cream puff when it is launched.
  • Impact star: 8-point white star (r 5.6 / 2.6, rounded 0.4, shade #fff3b5), life 0.14 s, scale 0.5 → 1.2 → 0.3; 60% chance of a 4-point white spark (shade #fff0a0) flying off with gravity 220.
  • White flash: overlay the white flash copy at min(0.85, flash*1.3) alpha and squash (sx *= 1 + 0.22f, sy *= 1 - 0.18f).
  • Pop-out burst: inflate the white flash sprite to 1.28× in 0.22 s with a ±10% wobble (sin(q*40)), then shrink to 0 by 0.5 s. Spawn 6 (large creature: 9) cloud puffs radially (5 merged ellipses, white/blue-white/orange variants, scale outBack in the first 20% then quadratic shrink, rising 7 u), 2–6 yellow stars (#ffd43f/#f39a1e) with gravity 180, and 4–10 colour chunks (6-point blobs in the figure's colours, outline 0.75 u) bouncing away. Large creatures add a shockwave ring.
  • Shockwave ring: flat ellipse (ry = 0.45·r), r = size·(4 + 26·outQuad(t)), stroked plum at width w + 1.4, then white/yellow at w, w = 2.4·(1 - t).
  • Dash smear: 3 white after-images at alpha 0.42/i, 7 u apart, figure stretched 1.2 × 0.88, cream dust puffs.
  • Celebration burst: yellow ring, 10 stars in a circle, 18 outlined confetti rectangles (0.45 u outline) in #ff6b6b #ffd43f #62c8ff #74e690 #c38bff #ff9e3d that flip by scaling y with cos(t*9).
  • Gem collectibles: rounded pentagon (roundPoly, r 0.55) #74e690 with a dark facet triangle #2fae66, a 35% plum girdle line, gloss + dot; the large gem is ×1.55 in blue #62c8ff / #2f83e0. Gems bob above their own small shadow; when collected they arc up 10 u with outBack and leave a small spark.
  • Orbiting lantern: a mini purple/cream root bulb with a carved glowing face plus an additive radial glow sprite (rgba(255,226,120,.75) → 0) pulsing at alpha 0.5±0.12.
  • Floating numbers: see Typography – baked Lilita One sprites, pop 0.3 → 1.35 → 1.0.
  • Ring of puffs: 10 orange puffs in a ring for the main character's own pop-out.

Atmosphere and lighting

  • No lighting system. Light is implied from the top-left by the cel crescent (always bottom right) and the gloss (always top left, pure white at 0.92 alpha).
  • Ground shadows: flat ellipses (main character 9.2×3.3 u at scale 1.1, small creature 7.7×2.8, ghost 5.8×2.1, large creature 18.5×5.8, round tree 16×5.2, pine 15×5, bush 12×3.8, rock 9.5×3, pumpkin 10×3.2, mushrooms 7×2.3, fence 18×2.6, sign 6×2, hay 14×4) collected into one path per frame and filled once with rgba(50,30,72,0.2) – overlapping shadows never stack darker. The main character's shadow shrinks while hopping; floating things (ghost, gems, lanterns, butterflies) have small detached shadows below them. Never grey, black or blurred.
  • Glow only for light sources: an additive (lighter) radial sprite behind lanterns; carved faces glow by colour alone (#ffd43f with a #fff3b0 core).
  • Ambient life: butterflies (4 pastel wing pairs #fff6d0/#f2d77a, #ffd1e4/#f39cbf, #d4ecff/#9cc9f2, #fff1a8/#f5c542, 0.55 u outline) drift at z 16 over the meadow with their own small shadows.
  • Overlays: none on the live scene. A menu overlay dims it with backdrop-filter: saturate(.2) brightness(.78) + a purple veil rgba(72,36,104,.6) and cream sunburst rays (repeating-conic-gradient, 9° stripes). An end-screen overlay goes grayscale(1) brightness(.62) blur(2px) under a purple radial vignette (rgba(40,18,58,.55) at the edges over rgba(70,40,105,.5)).

UI

Chunky toy HUD in HTML/CSS: cream pills and plum troughs, 4 px plum borders, big radii and a solid 4–5 px plum drop (box-shadow: 0 5px 0) instead of blurred shadows. Bars get a 3-stop vertical gradient and a white gloss strip. Counters use Lilita One with the sticker outline, labels Fredoka 600 in muted mauve #8a6280. Icons are drawn with the same part painter on a 48 px grid with a 2.6 px outline (heart, root bulb, lightning, pot, basket...). Choice cards have a coloured header band (orange, mint, violet) and pop in with overshoot easing.

Motion and animation

  • Main character hops rather than walks: phase = anim*0.42, f = fract(phase), air = sin(f*PI), lift 3.4·air u. Contact factor max(0, 1 - min(f, 1-f)*6): sy = 1 + .07air - .13contact, sx = 1 - .04air + .12contact. Frame 2 in the air, frames 1/3 alternate on landings, tilt 0.07·facing·air rad, a dust puff behind on every landing.
  • Idle: breathing sy = 1 + .03 sin(3.2t), sx = 1 - .022 sin(3.2t). A launch squashes 5%, a celebration wobbles ±18%, a flinch squashes 16% with the pained face.
  • Small creature: 4 frames at anim*0.85, rock ±0.1 rad, bob 1.3 u, 5–6% squash on contact, per-instance random scale 0.94–1.06.
  • Ghost: floats 4.5 u up, bob ±1.6 u at 2.6t, hem frames at 7 fps, 4% wobble at 5.2t, lean up to 0.16 rad into its velocity.
  • Large creature: anim*0.32, lift 2 u, cubic contact squash 8%, rock 0.035 rad.
  • Appearing: ground creatures stretch out of the dirt hole with outElastic; ghosts fade in + outBack.
  • Collectibles bob 1.1 u at 5t with 4–5% squash; collected gems arc up 10 u with outBack.
  • Props: trees and bushes sway by skewing around the base (0.025 / 0.035 at 1.3t).
  • Floating numbers: scale 0.3 → 1.35 (first 10%) → 1.0, shrink 70% and fade in the last 28%.
  • Overshoot easing everywhere: outBack, outElastic, CSS cubic-bezier(.3,1.7,.5,1).

Do and don't

Do

  • Use one outline colour #3a2240, drawn as a separate pass so parts merge into one silhouette.
  • Give every shape exactly one cel crescent (self-clip offset up-left) and one white gloss at the top-left.
  • Build from fat rounded primitives: ellipses, capsules, Catmull-Rom blobs, rounded polygons.
  • Bake sprites once; only transform them at draw time.
  • Squash, stretch and overshoot everything.
  • Keep the ground flat, pastel and outline-free with crisp region edges.
  • Batch ground shadows into one translucent plum fill.
  • Pre-bake white flash copies for flash feedback.

Don't

  • Don't use black or per-object outline colours or random line weights.
  • Don't add gradients or textures to character bodies.
  • Don't draw thin, spiky, realistic anatomy or hard 90° corners.
  • Don't outline ground regions or decals.
  • Don't use blurred or grey shadows; UI shadows are solid plum lips.
  • Don't re-path hundreds of figures every frame.
  • Don't make the ground as saturated as the characters.

Implementation recipe

  1. Set up a world-unit camera: scale = canvasHeight / 360; the main character is ~25 × 37 units.
  2. Define the palette as fill/shade pairs plus OUT = '#3a2240', OL = 1.15, IL = 0.55.
  3. Implement the Painter (outline / fill / flash modes) and gloss() exactly as in the Code section, plus shape helpers E, CAP, blobPath, roundPoly, leafPath.
  4. Implement build(box, fn, mode) that creates a canvas of (box size) * K, scales by K, runs fn with an outline painter and then with a fill (or flash) painter.
  5. Write each figure and prop as a part list, back to front: tail/back limbs → body → front limbs → head → face details → top decorations. Give every big part shade, sh and hi.
  6. Make 4 frames per figure by varying limb endpoints and small offsets (feet, arm angles, leaf sway, hem phase); bake normal + flash for each.
  7. Generate the ground: noise fields → per-pixel signed distance → coverage + rim blend → flat decals. Cache it in chunks.
  8. Per frame: ground, one batched shadow path, y-sort figures and props, blit with translate · rotate · scale(sx·facing, sy) around the feet, then particles and numbers.
  9. Add the motion rules (hop, waddle, float, stomp, overshoot pops) and the effects (impact star, flash, puff burst, stars, chunks, ring).
  10. Build the HUD in HTML/CSS with the panel/bar/card styles above; draw icons with the same painter on a 48 px grid and a 2.6 px outline.

1. The two-pass part painter (outline merge + cel crescent + gloss)

const OUT = '#3a2240', OL = 1.15, FLASH_SH = '#efe6f6', TAU = Math.PI * 2;
function Painter(ctx, mode, ol) { this.ctx = ctx; this.mode = mode; this.ol = ol; }
Painter.prototype.part = function (shape, fill, o = {}) {
  const ctx = this.ctx;
  if (this.mode === 'outline') {              // pass 1: everything plum, stroke at 2x weight
    ctx.beginPath(); shape(ctx);
    ctx.lineWidth = (o.ol != null ? o.ol : this.ol) * 2;
    ctx.strokeStyle = OUT; ctx.fillStyle = OUT; ctx.stroke(); ctx.fill();
    return;
  }
  const flash = this.mode === 'flash';
  ctx.save(); ctx.beginPath(); shape(ctx);
  if (o.inner) { ctx.lineWidth = o.inner * 2; ctx.strokeStyle = OUT; ctx.stroke(); } // inner line
  ctx.clip();
  const paint = (shade) => {
    ctx.fillStyle = flash ? (shade ? FLASH_SH : '#fff') : (shade ? o.shade || fill : fill);
    ctx.fillRect(-500, -500, 1000, 1000);
  };
  if (o.sh) {                                  // shade everywhere, lit colour on the shifted shape
    paint(true);
    ctx.save(); ctx.translate(o.sh[0], o.sh[1]); ctx.beginPath(); shape(ctx); ctx.restore();
    ctx.clip(); paint(false);
  } else paint(false);
  ctx.restore();
  if (!flash && o.hi) {                         // gloss: white ellipse at the top-left
    ctx.save(); ctx.beginPath(); shape(ctx); ctx.clip();
    ctx.globalAlpha = 0.92; ctx.fillStyle = '#fff';
    ctx.beginPath(); ctx.ellipse(o.hi[0], o.hi[1], o.hi[2], o.hi[3], o.hi[4] || 0, 0, TAU); ctx.fill();
    ctx.restore();
  }
};

2. Baking a sprite and blitting it with squash & stretch

let K = 3; // canvas px per world unit
function build(box, fn, mode) {
  const cv = document.createElement('canvas');
  cv.width = Math.ceil((box[2] - box[0]) * K); cv.height = Math.ceil((box[3] - box[1]) * K);
  const ctx = cv.getContext('2d');
  ctx.scale(K, K); ctx.translate(-box[0], -box[1]);
  ctx.lineJoin = 'round'; ctx.lineCap = 'round';
  fn(new Painter(ctx, 'outline', OL));
  fn(new Painter(ctx, mode || 'fill', OL));
  return { c: cv, ox: -box[0] * K, oy: -box[1] * K };
}
function blit(ctx, s, x, y, sx, sy, rot = 0, facing = 1) { // pivot = feet
  ctx.save(); ctx.translate(x, y); if (rot) ctx.rotate(rot); ctx.scale(sx * facing, sy);
  ctx.drawImage(s.c, -s.ox / K, -s.oy / K, s.c.width / K, s.c.height / K);
  ctx.restore();
}
// example prop: a bush = 4 ellipses as ONE part
const E = (x, y, rx, ry) => (c) => { c.moveTo(x + rx, y); c.ellipse(x, y, rx, ry, 0, 0, TAU); };
const bush = build([-15, -20, 15, 2], (P) => P.part(
  (c) => { E(-6.4, -6, 6.4, 6)(c); E(6, -5.8, 6, 5.6)(c); E(0, -9.6, 7.6, 7.2)(c); E(-0.5, -4.6, 7, 4.8)(c); },
  '#73c257', { shade: '#4e9a40', sh: [-2.1, -2.0], hi: [-4.8, -13, 2.2, 1.1, -0.6] }));

3. Carved glowing face and two-tone body fill

function carve(c, pathFn, dx = 0.2, dy = 0.7) {   // dark rim + glowing inside shifted down
  c.save();
  c.beginPath(); pathFn(c); c.fillStyle = '#8a3214'; c.fill(); c.clip();
  c.translate(dx, dy); c.beginPath(); pathFn(c); c.fillStyle = '#ffd43f'; c.fill();
  c.restore();
}
// fill function for P.part: cream bottom, purple top, wavy split (root-bulb creature);
// shade=true gives the shade pair
const turnipFill = (col, waveY, amp, freq) => (c, shade) => {
  c.fillStyle = shade ? col.botSh : col.bot; c.fillRect(-500, -500, 1000, 1000);
  c.fillStyle = shade ? col.topSh : col.top;
  c.beginPath(); c.moveTo(-60, -120); c.lineTo(60, -120); c.lineTo(60, waveY);

4. Ground: crisp region edges with an inner rim from a sampled noise field

// F: field values on a grid every `cpx` pixels (value > 0 = inside). Returns [r,g,b] for one pixel.
function shadePixel(F, nx, px, py, cpx, base, layer /* {f:[r,g,b], r:[r,g,b], rimPx} */) {
  const gx = Math.floor(px / cpx), gy = Math.floor(py / cpx), u = px / cpx - gx, v = py / cpx - gy;
  const A = F[gy * nx + gx], B = F[gy * nx + gx + 1], C = F[(gy + 1) * nx + gx], D = F[(gy + 1) * nx + gx + 1];
  const s = (A + (B - A) * u) * (1 - v) + (C + (D - C) * u) * v;
  const du = (B - A) * (1 - v) + (D - C) * v, dv = (C - A) * (1 - u) + (D - B) * u;
  const g = Math.sqrt(du * du + dv * dv) / cpx;
  const dist = g > 1e-9 ? s / g : (s > 0 ? 99 : -99);          // signed distance in pixels
  const cov = Math.min(1, Math.max(0, dist + 0.5));              // 1-px anti-aliased edge
  const rim = Math.min(1, Math.max(0, layer.rimPx - dist + 0.5)); // darker band just inside
  return base.map((b, i) => {
    const lay = layer.f[i] + (layer.r[i] - layer.f[i]) * rim;
    return b + (lay - b) * cov;
  });
}

5. Batched shadows and sticker floating numbers

function drawShadows(ctx, items) {                 // one path, one fill -> no darker overlaps
  ctx.beginPath();
  for (const a of items) { ctx.moveTo(a.x + a.rx, a.y); ctx.ellipse(a.x, a.y, a.rx, a.ry, 0, 0, Math.PI * 2); }
  ctx.fillStyle = 'rgba(50,30,72,0.2)'; ctx.fill();
}
// floating number: plum outline + plum drop, white or (highlighted) yellow -> orange gradient face
function damageNumber(ctx, x, y, txt, crit) {
  const fs = crit ? 12.5 : 9;
  ctx.font = fs + 'px "Lilita One", "Fredoka", sans-serif';
  ctx.textAlign = 'center'; ctx.textBaseline = 'middle'; ctx.lineJoin = 'round';
  ctx.strokeStyle = '#3a2240'; ctx.lineWidth = fs * 0.3;
  ctx.strokeText(txt, x, y + fs * 0.09); ctx.fillStyle = '#3a2240'; ctx.fillText(txt, x, y + fs * 0.09);
  ctx.strokeText(txt, x, y);
  if (crit) { const g = ctx.createLinearGradient(0, y - fs * 0.45, 0, y + fs * 0.4);
    g.addColorStop(0, '#fff27a'); g.addColorStop(0.5, '#ffc93a'); g.addColorStop(1, '#ff8a2a'); ctx.fillStyle = g; }
  else ctx.fillStyle = '#fff';
  ctx.fillText(txt, x, y);
}

Adapting it to your project

  • Keep fixed: the single plum outline and its weights (1.15 / 0.55 u), the two-pass painter, exactly one crescent + one gloss per shape, top-left light, flat pastel ground with rim bands, batched plum shadows, overshoot motion, Lilita One + Fredoka, the solid-lip UI.
  • May change: all fill/shade pairs. For a re-theme (e.g. sci-fi or underwater) pick saturated warm colours for the main character, cool or contrasting ones for creatures and a pastel, low-contrast ground (sea-foam, pale sand, light slate). Keep each shade ~20% darker than its fill and keep the outline dark plum – or switch it globally to another very dark, slightly coloured ink (e.g. deep navy #22264a), never black.
  • Mixing elements: the characters and props carry the look on their own (plum outline + crescent + gloss), so they sit well on another style's ground as long as that ground is calmer and less saturated than they are. When using only this world, keep it outline-free so other figures stay in front.
  • Other genres: side-view platformers – reuse the same sprites, keep the hop squash; puzzle or card projects – use the painter for tiles and icons (48 px grid, 2.6 px outline).
  • Other resolutions: only K changes (3–6 px per unit); outlines scale with world units so the look is identical on phones and 4K.

Performance notes

  • Everything is baked once at init: 4 frames × (normal + flash) × 2 facings per figure, ~60 canvases in total; per frame only drawImage with transforms. 300+ figures stay well under budget.
  • Ground: 256-unit chunks generated incrementally; noise is sampled on a 4-unit grid and the per-pixel distance work runs only in grid cells that touch an edge (others are flat-filled with Uint32Array.fill). Decals are baked into the chunk. Cap ground resolution at 3 px/unit.
  • Shadows: one path + one fill per frame. Floating numbers: one cached sprite per value (cache cleared at 500 entries; re-baked once Lilita One has loaded).
  • Hot spots: chunk generation during fast movement and very large particle bursts; keep puffs and chunks as baked sprites, never as live paths.
