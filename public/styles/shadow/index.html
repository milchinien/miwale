Shadow Theatre — Game Art Style Spec

Backlit paper shadow-puppet theatre: indigo-black cut-paper silhouettes over glowing teal, violet, crimson and amber light fields.

The screen is a lit puppet stage seen from above. The floor is the light: big organic colour fields (moonlit teal, dusk violet, crimson, amber) glow like a backlit paper screen. They are joined by thin ink seams threaded with lace beads and covered in flat ink cut-outs: grass tufts, leaves, filigree scrolls, dandelion clocks and stars. Everything that stands is a flat silhouette in indigo-black ink #0b0510, built from tapered strokes, lens leaves and spiral curls in the tradition of Lotte Reiniger's cut-paper silhouette films. The only colour on a silhouette is emissive: the main character's carved, glowing pumpkin face, the small creatures' acid-green eyes, the large creature's magenta cracks. There is also a soft baked rim of light. Veil-like ghost creatures invert the rule and are pale glowing veils. Velvet curtains, a scalloped valance and a vignette frame the scene as a theatre.

When to use it

  • Great for crowded scenes. A flat silhouette reads at any size, and 200 black shapes on a glowing floor stay legible. When the screen fills with ink, the emissive eyes still separate the creature types.
  • Halloween, folklore, fairy tales, gothic storybooks. The puppet-theatre framing adds narrative charm for free.
  • Silhouette platformers and puzzle games. The light/ink split is the whole visual language.
  • Bad for colour-coded designs. Team colours, element types or equipment cannot show on bodies; only small emissive marks are allowed.
  • Bad for daylight, sci-fi or "realistic" settings, and for projects whose characters need readable faces or costumes.
  • Bad for tiny units in dense strategy views. Silhouettes under ~12 px tall lose their identifying curls.

Palette rules

  • There are only two materials: light (the floor, glows and emissive parts) and ink (everything standing). No mid-tones on bodies.
  • Silhouettes are always #0b0510 at full opacity. Never tint a body in a field colour, and never use pure #000.
  • Character colour must be small and emissive. One emissive hue per character class: main character orange-gold, small creatures acid green, large creatures magenta, ghosts cold blue-white.
  • Fields always neighbour in the order teal | violet | crimson | amber, because they are thresholds of one noise value. Target roughly 40 % amber, 30 % crimson, 20 % violet and 10 % teal on screen.
  • Warm light (gold/orange) belongs to the main character and friendly objects (lanterns, lit gourd lanterns, the follow-spot).

Rendering technique

The world is drawn in world units with 360 units visible vertically. The main character is about 18 units wide and 33 tall.

  1. Init: pick the bake resolution. K = clamp(round(screenHeightPx / 360 × 1.3 × 4) / 4, 2.5, 5) px per world unit (DPR is capped at 2). The extra 1.3 keeps silhouettes crisp when they are slightly scaled.
  2. Bake every sprite with mk(x0, y0, x1, y1, draw, pad). It creates a canvas of (x1 − x0 + 2·pad) × K px and sets the transform to K. Fill and stroke are #0b0510 with round caps and joins. The draw function then paints in world units with feet at (0, 0) and the figure facing +x.
  3. Per sprite, also bake:
    • a mirrored copy, for facing −x;
    • a rim-light copy rimSpr(s, colour, rad): tint the silhouette, stamp it 8 times around a circle of d = rad·K·0.55 px, blur by d, then add the unblurred stamps at 60 %;
    • for the main character, a white "hurt" rim (rad 1.1);
    • for creatures, torn-paper shard sets for the break-up animation.
  4. Bake glows as radial-gradient squares: glowSpr(radius, stops). Examples: the warm pool (r 30), the face halo (r 9), gold (r 10), pink (r 12), cyan (r 6), embers (r 3) and the dark contact shadow (r 10, rgba(14,5,20,.55)).
  5. Ground chunks of 256 world units are rendered by a generator (see World and ground) at up to 2.5 px per unit, then cached.
  6. Per frame, in order:
    • ground chunks;
    • contact shadows and the main character's additive follow-spot;
    • props, creatures, main character, collectibles, orbiting objects and projectiles, all y-sorted;
    • particles;
    • pop-up numbers;
    • the theatre-frame overlay, baked once per screen size.
  7. Light is always additive. Glows, rims, embers, flares and trails use globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'. Ink is drawn with normal source-over.
  8. Blit with world-unit sizes. Draw with drawImage(c, x − ax/K, y − ay/K, w/K, h/K), and snap positions to device pixels when not rotating. Wobble and sway use a rotate transform around the feet.

World and ground

The floor is a light field shader, computed per pixel from one scalar noise value. It is the brightest, most colourful layer of the picture; everything standing on it is flat ink #0b0510.

f(x, y) = (warped(x/1300, y/1300, seed 501, warp 0.85, 3 oct) − 0.5) × 9.5 + (fbm(x/420, y/420, seed 502, 2 oct) − 0.5) × 0.9
  • Bands. The thresholds TH = [−0.95, −0.3, 0.35] split f into teal | violet | crimson | amber (edge → core: teal #2c748c → #76ccc6, violet #6840a2 → #b682dc, crimson #b02a42 → #f26a52, amber #ea7a2c → #ffc662). Neighbouring bands blend over ±0.075 f-units (smoothstep).
  • Colour inside a band. Take the depth into the band (its distance to the nearest threshold) and compute t = smoothstep(0, 0.75, depth) × (0.55 + 0.45·gloss), where gloss = fbm(x/520, y/520, 503, 3). The colour lerps from the edge colour towards the core colour by t. Amber also lerps towards the hot #fff0c4 by smoothstep(0.9, 2.2, depth) × (0.25 + 0.4·gloss). The result is finally multiplied by 0.9 + 0.2·gloss.
  • Sampling grid. f, gloss, colour, teal weight, ripple phase and a signed distance to the nearest seam are sampled on a 4-unit grid with an 8-unit margin. The distance is sd = (f − nearestThreshold) / |∇f|, clamped to ±60 units. Pixels interpolate the grid bilinearly.
  • Light bleed. Where |sd| < 6, lerp towards rgb(255,240,215) by (1 − |sd|/6)² × 0.22. Each seam looks like a slit where light leaks through.
  • Moonlit ripples. Only on teal: phase = y·0.72 + 7·perlin(x/95, y/95) + 2.5·perlin(x/33, y/33). Where sin(phase) > 0.86, lerp towards rgb(215,255,245) by (sin − 0.86) × 7.1 × tealWeight × 0.42. This gives thin wavy horizontal highlight lines.
  • Paper grain. A 256² Int8 tile: a 3×3 box sum of hash noise × 3.2 plus the raw value × 5, plus 70 random-walk fibres of 8–34 px (+4 each). It is added as R +g, G +0.9g, B +0.8g.
  • Seam line. a = (0.36 − |sd|)·res + 0.5. Where a > 0, lerp towards #16081c by min(1, a) × 0.85. The line is about 0.72 units wide and anti-aliased.
  • Lace layer. These are ink vector paths drawn into a separate canvas, then composited onto the chunk once at 80 % alpha, so the light glows faintly through.
    • Seam beads. Points are scattered every 4.2 units and kept where |sd| < 2.4. Each point is projected onto the seam (p − n·sd) and offset 1.1–1.5 units to one side. It becomes one of three motifs:
      • 45 %: a dot (r 0.62) inside a ring (r 1.25, 0.32-unit stroke);
      • 35 %: a pair of lens leaves (2.3 long);
      • 20 %: two dots (r 0.42 and 0.3).
    • Field decals. Each decal kind has its own scatter cell size, and decals are skipped within 0.06 f-units of a seam:
      • amber: grass tufts (cell 15), 5–9 tapered blades 3.5–8 units long, density raised by a noise mask; dandelion clocks (14 radial seeds on a stem);
      • crimson: filigree double scrolls (cell 30, 62 %, spirals 9–15 units with leaves) and lace rosettes (cell 64);
      • violet: small fallen leaves and maple shapes (cell 15/34) and 4–5-point cut-out stars plus additive light glints (cell 17);
      • teal: broken ripple ellipses (2–3 rings, radius 3.5 + 3.2k, height × 0.4, dashed in random gaps), reeds with cattail heads, lily pads;
      • large fern rosettes and branching root systems (cell 165).
    • Clumps, not sprinkle. Every field decal is multiplied by a clump mask keep = smoothstep(0.45, 0.68, noise(x/150, y/150)): e.g. crimson filigree 5 % + 60 %·keep, violet stars 4 % + 40 %·keep, amber grass 1.2 % + 72 %·mask. Big parts of every light field stay clean, so the figures read against calm light.
  • Additive glints. These go on top of the lace. Violet gets 4-point stars in rgba(255,236,190,.55) with a rgba(255,250,230,.7) dot. Teal ripples get pale ellipses in rgba(220,255,250,.35).

Scale: fields are hundreds of units across (noise period about 1300 units), so one screen shows 2–4 fields. Decals are tiny: 2–15 units, with strokes of 0.25–0.85 units. Keep decals much finer than the standing figures so the floor never competes with them.

Characters

All characters are shadow puppets: flat #0b0510 silhouettes with no outline and no shading, built from ellipses, tapered limbs (Catmull-Rom-smoothed polylines filled as ribbons), lens leaves, spiral curls and cut-out holes. They face +x and are mirrored for −x. Every tip (toes, tails, stems, crowns) ends in a curl or a leaf. Faces are holes or emissive shapes, never painted features. Each character class has a unique outline and exactly one emissive colour.

The main character (a slim puppet figure with a round pumpkin head, a jagged cloak and a streaming scarf) measures about −14…9 units in x and −33…1.5 units in y, with the feet at the origin. Draw order, back to front:

  1. Back leg. A jointed puppet leg: hip → knee (4.2 units) → foot (4.4 units) as a limb 1.15 → 0.9 wide. The foot is a 1.4 → 0.7 limb ending in a toe curl (2.2 long, turn −5.5).
  2. Back arm. Shoulder at (−1.6, sh + 0.8), upper arm 3.4, forearm 3.2, a 1.0-unit limb ending in a hand circle (r 0.85).
  3. Front leg.
  4. Cloak. From the shoulders (y = −16.2 + bob) to a jagged hem of 7 zig-zag points alternating +1.3 / −0.7. The hem trails backwards by 2.2 units when walking. A small clasp curl sits at the back edge.
  5. Scarf. An ellipse (3.5 × 1.3) plus two streaming tails: limbs 1.6 → 0.5, 11.6 units long when walking, hanging down when idle, waving at twice the step frequency.
  6. Front arm. Width 1.1.
  7. Pumpkin head. Four overlapping ellipses (pumpkinShape, rx 6.8, ry 5.4) at (0.8, −22.4). Add a stem limb (1.5 → 0.9) curving back, a leaf (4.2 long) and a long spiral curl (4.6, turn 6).
  8. Puppet construction, punched last with destination-out so the light behind the screen shines through: a rivet hole (r 0.36) at every pivot – both knees, the front elbow and the shoulder – and pierced lace in the cloak: four diamonds (0.9 × 1.5) in a row 2 units above the hem and a crescent moon (two circles r 1.25 / 1.05 offset by (0.55, −0.3), even-odd) in the middle of the cloak. They give the big calm cloak shape a second level of detail without breaking the silhouette.
  9. Carved face, the brightest thing on screen. Two triangular eyes, a triangle nose and a zig-zag grin with two teeth. It is filled by glowFill with the radial FACE gradient (#fffbe2 → #ffd65e → #ff9a22 → #ff6a10, centre radius 6) and a 1.6-unit bloom in rgba(255,150,40,.95).

Frames. There are 6 walk frames (phase i/6) and 4 idle frames, each baked with a warm rim (#ffb24a, rad 1.3), a white hurt rim and mirrored copies, plus a crouch (the idle pose squashed 1.08 × 0.86 on its pivots) and a dash lean (walk phase 0.25 rotated 0.24 rad around the hip point (0, −8.6)), both with their own warm rim. The walk bob is −|cos(phase)| × 0.9, and the head bobs separately with a 0.4 phase lag.

Per frame:

  • an additive follow-spot pool (r 30, scaled 1.1 × 0.62, alpha 0.55 ± 0.05);
  • a contact shadow (0.8 × 0.26);
  • the rim at 75 % ('lighter');
  • the sprite;
  • an additive face halo (r 9) at the head position, flickering 0.8 + 0.12·sin(11t) + 0.08·sin(27t).

The main character is always rim-lit and stands in its warm follow-spot, so it wins against any crowd of creatures.

Small creature (a purple root-bulb puppet with a leafy filigree crown, about 24 × 30 units):

  • body: a bulb ellipse (7.8 × 7.0, or 7.0 × 7.6 for the variant) with a little top knob;
  • limbs: two jointed root legs ending in curls, a taproot tail curling behind, curled arms, and root hairs;
  • filigree leaf crown: 3–4 curved stems (curlPts, 6.4–8.6 long) with a large leaf at each tip and two side leaves;
  • mouth: a zig-zag mouth cut out with destination-out, so the floor light shows through;
  • eyes: two angry slanted glowing eyes in acid green (#ffffe0 → #d8ff62 → #7fe02a, bloom 1.3 in rgba(170,255,70,.95)).

It has 2 variants × 6 frames and rocks ±0.075 rad as it walks.

Fast creature / ghost:

  • the inversion of the rule: a white veil (head ellipse 5 × 5.6, a ragged lower edge, three curled tail wisps and two reaching arms with three long fingers);
  • colouring: tinted source-atop with a vertical gradient #fbfdff → #dcefff → #a9d4ff, then faded towards the tail with destination-in (95 % → 35 % alpha);
  • face: hollow eye and mouth ellipses in #1c0f36;
  • halos: the sprite is composited over a dark halo (tint #160828, blur 2.2·K px, alpha 0.42) and then a light halo (tint #d6f0ff, blur 1.4·K px, alpha 0.95), so it glows on any field.

It has 4 frames and hovers. Keep ghosts cold (blue-white) so they never compete with the warm face of the main character.

Large creature (a hulking root-bulb colossus, about 44 × 62 units):

  • body: a knobby bulb (radius 15 × 14 modulated by 1 + 0.07·sin(5a) + 0.05·sin(9a));
  • limbs: massive gorilla arms (5.2 → 2.6) with claws, and thick legs;
  • crown and beard: an antler crown of five thick curled stems with big leaves, and a dangling root beard;
  • emissive: magenta cracks, stroked #ffb8f0 at 0.5–0.7 units with shadowBlur 1.6·K in rgba(255,70,210,1), a diamond core and slit eyes in the same magenta (#fff → #ffc2f2 / #ffd0f6 → #ff5ad2);
  • extra glow: an additive pink glow (r 12) on the chest pulses at runtime.

It has 6 frames and rocks ±0.035 rad.

Readability in crowds. Bodies all merge into one ink mass, so identity comes from:

  1. the outline features that stick out (crowns, veils, antlers);
  2. the emissive colour code (green dots = small creatures, magenta = large creatures, white = ghosts, gold = main character);
  3. the main character's constant warm rim and spotlight.

Test every new silhouette at 30 px tall in ink only. Creatures only get a rim during an impact flash (cream #fff6dc, capped at 0.9, or 0.7 for large creatures). Never give them a permanent rim.

Props

Props are standing ink silhouettes in #0b0510, baked once at K px per world unit and y-sorted together with the characters. They use the same vocabulary as the characters — tapered limbs, lens leaves, spiral curls, destination-out holes where light shows through — and no outlines or shading. Light on props is only emissive (lantern panes, carved faces) plus additive glows.

  • Trees (sprite box −58…58 × −114…4 units, 3 variants + mirrored copies):
    • five roots (limbs 2.4 → 0.3, reaching 6–12 units out, half of them ending in curls);
    • a leaning trunk (lean ±0.12) as a 6.2 → 3.2 limb up to y ≈ −46;
    • recursive branching, three depths, 3–4 crown branches 17–21 units long plus two side boughs, branches splitting into 2–3 at 0.62–0.76 of the length, tips ending in curls, about 4 leaves per tip (2.2–3.6 units);
    • a cut-out knothole (1.0 × 1.9 ellipse at y −19) so the floor light shows through; one variant carries a small owl silhouette with ear tufts instead.
  • Iron gates (42 × 39 units): two pillars 4.8 wide with cut-out arched windows and ball-and-spike caps, spear-tipped bars every 2.6 units on an arched top (height 17 + 7·cos), 0.8-unit rails, a ring lace band (rings r 1.1), four scrolls and a 4-point star at the crown.
  • Fences (34 × 21 units, 2 variants): 7 slightly tilted spear pickets (0.8 wide, 14 tall, end posts with ball tops), two 0.7-unit rails, pairs of S-curls between the pickets, and grass blades at the base.
  • Graves (16 × 25 units, 3 kinds, all with a grass base):
    • a Celtic cross with a see-through ring (r 4.1, 1.3-unit stroke);
    • a round-topped stone with a cut-out crescent-moon window and a scalloped rim of 9 beads;
    • a crooked wooden cross with a leaf wreath (9 leaves on a ring of r 3).
  • Scarecrow (38 × 54 units): a pole and crossbar, a coat with a jagged hem, sleeves with tattered strips and straw, a round head with a wide-brimmed bent hat, and a crow perched on one arm.
  • Crow posts (16 × 30 units): a post with a crow on top, 2 frames (head turned / wing flick).
  • Lantern poles (−5…11 × −40…1 units): a pole ending in a curled hook, two side curls, a short chain and a hanging lantern whose pane glows #ffffe6 → #ffe08a → #ffaa3a (bloom 1.2, rgba(255,200,90,1)) with an ink cross of window bars, plus an additive gold glow (r 9) at runtime.
  • Lit gourd lanterns (34 × 16 units, 3 variants + mirrored): one to three pumpkins (pumpkinShape, r 3.4–6.2) on curly ground vines with three-lobed leaves; the lit ones have a carved face in the FACE gradient (bloom 1.1) and an additive flickering gold glow of radius 1.8 × r.
  • Weeds (16 × 18 units, 3 variants): thistles (three stems with a bulb head and 7 spines), tall grass clumps (11 blades 6–14 tall with seed heads), fiddlehead ferns (four stems ending in tight curls).

Placement. Props are scattered on a cell grid and filtered by field: trees in 118-unit cells with a field-dependent chance (teal 40 %, violet 50 %, crimson 30 %, amber 14 %); graves cluster where a low-frequency noise (x/330) is above 0.64 and never on amber; fences (zone 0.56–0.62) and gates (zone above 0.62) in 150-unit cells; scarecrows, crow posts and gourd lanterns only on the warm crimson and amber fields; lantern poles anywhere (135-unit cells, 22 %); weeds in 44-unit cells as thickets (2 % + 75 %·smoothstep(0.45, 0.7, noise(x/200))), grass on teal, ferns on violet, thistles or grass on warm fields; gourd lanterns in patches (4 % + 55 % by a 260-unit noise). Props stay away from seams.

Motion. Trees sway ±0.012 rad and scarecrows ±0.02 rad at 0.7 rad/s, hinged at the foot like puppets. Weeds sway ±0.05 rad at 1.3 rad/s. The crow flicks its head on every 4th beat (0.8 Hz). Lanterns and gourd lanterns flicker with 0.75 + 0.15·sin(9t) + 0.1·sin(23t).

Shadows. Every prop gets a squashed dark contact shadow (rgba(14,5,20,.55) → 0) whose width comes from the prop's sh value: trees 1.9, gates 1.8, fences 1.4, scarecrows 1.0, gourd lanterns 0.9, graves 0.75, weeds 0.6, crow posts and lantern poles 0.5.

Effects and particles

All effects are either light (additive, 'lighter') or paper (flat ink). Nothing is mid-tone.

  • Projectile (a glowing seed):
    • a baked lens shape with a gradient #ffcf7a → #fff6d8 → #fff and a 1.2-unit bloom;
    • rotated along its velocity with a spin wobble of sin(spin) × 0.6;
    • an additive tapered trail 11 units long (rgba(255,120,30,0) → rgba(255,235,170,.9)) that grows in over the first 0.125 s;
    • an ember glow at 70 %, scaled 1.4.
  • Impact:
    • 3 additive spark streaks (7-unit lens, transparent orange → rgba(255,250,220,1)), 0.22–0.32 s;
    • a 4-point flare (arms 9 and 6 units, lens leaves with gradients, plus a radial core) for 0.16 s;
    • the creature's cream rim flash.
  • Paper-crumble break-up (when a creature disappears):
    • for the first 0.14 s, the rim at 90 % over the sprite;
    • then the pre-cut torn shards (5 for small creatures, 4 for ghosts, 7 for large creatures) fly outward by 2 + 10·outQuad(u) units, rise 5 units, then fall 22·u², rotate ±(1.4–2.2)·u, shrink to 70 % and fade with 1 − u²;
    • each shard's torn edge glows #ff9a3a (0.9 units, source-atop);
    • paper-scrap particles (4–6-gon ink chips with one ember-lit edge) tumble with gravity 160;
    • embers rise;
    • a flare (size 1.1, or 2.4 for large creatures).
  • Ghost dissolve: 5 pale curled wisps drift up, and cold embers.
  • Embers: 3-unit additive radial sprites (white → rgba(255,190,80,.85) → transparent) rising (gravity −18, drag 2.2), flickering 0.7 + 0.3·sin(30t + seed). There are warm, cold and pink variants.
  • Collectibles:
    • small gems are ink teardrops with an aqua glass interior (#fff → #b8fff4 → #3fe0d8) and an additive cyan glow (r 6);
    • large gems are gold (#fff0a8 → #ffb03a) with a curl on top and a rotating 4-point flare;
    • when collected: one small ember (cold for small gems, warm for large ones).
  • Orbiting object (a small lantern): an ink bulb (5.2 × 4.8) with a leaf top and a curl, a gold glowing carved face (#ffffe8 → #ffe27a → #ffaa2a, bloom 1.3), plus an additive gold glow; it tilts ±0.12 rad as it circles.
  • Afterimage streak: three ink-tinted copies of the current frame (rgba(20,6,26,1), alpha 0.4/i, 6 units apart) and an ember trail.
  • Ring burst:
    • a light ring (r 10.5) with 12 small stars, growing 0.4 + 1.2·outQuad(age);
    • a large flare (2.8);
    • 14 embers;
    • a rotating flare above the main character's head while the burst lasts.
  • Rise-in entrance: a creature rises from a stage slot like a rod puppet. It uses outBack(t × 1.15) from 95 % of its height below, is clipped at the floor, shows a visible 0.7-unit ink rod, gets an additive warm slot glow and swings by sin(14t)·(1 − t)·0.25 rad.

Atmosphere and lighting

The scene is lit from below and behind the paper — the glowing floor is the light source. Standing objects are flat ink #0b0510; all extra light is added with globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter', never by brightening ink.

  • There is no directional light and no cast sun shadow.
  • Contact shadows are a dark radial sprite (r 10, rgba(14,5,20,.55) → rgba(14,5,20,.28) at 55 % → 0), squashed to about 0.3 of its width under each figure. Prop shadow width comes from the prop's sh value, e.g. 1.9 for trees and 0.5 for crow posts.
  • Follow-spot: an additive warm pool under the main character (r 30, rgba(255,214,140,.55) centre → rgba(255,170,90,.22) at 50 % → transparent), scaled 1.1 × 0.62.
  • Rim light: the main character carries a permanent warm rim (#ffb24a, rad 1.3, added at 75 %); creatures get a cream rim (#fff6dc) only during an impact flash. Rims are 8 tinted offset stamps, blurred, drawn behind the ink sprite.
  • Seam light: every seam between the floor fields leaks light 6 units out towards rgb(255,240,215), like slits in a lit paper screen.
  • Emissive bloom is baked: glowFill draws a gradient path once with shadowBlur = bloom·K and then again sharp. Additive glow sprites flicker around faces and lanterns at runtime (0.8 + 0.12·sin(11t) + 0.08·sin(27t)).
  • Theatre frame overlay, baked per screen size:
    • a radial vignette centred at (W/2, 0.52H), from 0.28·max(W, H) to 0.72·max(W, H): rgba(14,4,22,0) → 0.22 at 60 % → 0.7 at the edge;
    • side curtains 3.4 % of the width with a bezier tie-back waist at 42 % height, velvet sheen gradient #0b0510 / #2a0a20 / #0b0510 / #240818 / #0b0510, and an ink tassel;
    • a scalloped valance (16 scallops, 3.2 % of the height) with tassel cords and triangles;
    • an ink stage lip gradient over the bottom 6 % (rgba(11,5,16,0) → 0.8).
  • No colour grading or blur at runtime. Everything is baked.

Motion and animation

Everything moves like a rod puppet: jointed limbs swing, and whole sprites rock around the feet.

  • Main character:
    • walk: 6 frames advanced at anim × 1.5 (≈ 9 fps); idle: 4 frames at 2.6 fps;
    • the legs swing ±0.6 rad and the knees bend by 0.55·max(0, sin(phase + side·1.4));
    • the cloak hem and scarf tails flutter at 2× the step frequency;
    • dash: the crouch frame while dashT > 0.165 (anticipation), then the lean pose with three ink afterimages;
    • hurt state: the white rim is added and the puppet tilts back on its rod by −facing × 0.2 × sin(1.5π·hurt) × hurt – one swing and one overshoot; while invulnerable the candle behind the screen flickers (the white rim at 60 % every other 1/12 s) – it never turns see-through.
  • Small creature: 6 frames at 4.8 per anim second, rocking ±0.075 rad, with a per-creature seed offset so crowds never march in sync.
  • Large creature: 6 frames at 3.6 per anim second, rocking ±0.035 rad, with feet lifted up to 3.6 units; the magenta chest glow pulses 0.45 ± 0.25 at 4 rad/s.
  • Ghost: 4 frames at 6 fps. It hovers 1.2 + 2.6·smoothstep(tri) units on a triangle wave with a 2.2 s period per ghost, so it lingers at the top and the bottom, and sways ±0.06 rad. It has no walk cycle.
  • Hit reaction: the cream rim flash plus a shove of 2.6 units (large creature 1.4) along the knock-back direction and a tilt of up to 0.22 rad away from the blow, both easing out with flash² over ~0.14 s.
  • Props: trees sway ±0.012 rad and scarecrows ±0.02 rad at 0.7 rad/s. Weeds sway ±0.05 rad at 1.3 rad/s. The crow flicks its head on every 4th beat (0.8 Hz). Lanterns and gourd lanterns flicker with 0.75 + 0.15·sin(9t) + 0.1·sin(23t).
  • Collectibles: bob ±0.8 units at 3 rad/s, the glow pulses 0.75–1.0 at 5 rad/s, and when collected they pop up to 1.6×.
  • Pop-up numbers: pop from 1.6× to 1× in 0.08 s and fade over the last 0.25 s.

Do and don't

Do

  • Make the floor the brightest, most colourful layer, and keep everything standing flat ink.
  • Build shapes from tapered limbs, lens leaves and spiral curls, and end every tip in a curl or leaf.
  • Give each creature class a unique silhouette feature and exactly one emissive colour.
  • Keep the main character permanently rim-lit in a warm follow-spot, because it must win against any crowd.
  • Carve holes (destination-out) for mouths, windows and knotholes so the light shows through.
  • Bake everything at init (frames, mirrors, rims, glows, shards) and only blit per frame.
  • Frame the scene as a stage: curtains, valance, vignette and stage lip.

Don't

  • Don't outline or gradient-shade silhouettes. The lit edge comes only from the additive rim copy.
  • Don't paint features in body colour, or give creatures warm emissives that compete with the main character's face.
  • Don't let lace decals rival the figures. Keep them fine (0.25–0.85-unit strokes) at 80 %.
  • Don't use pure black, grey or photo textures. Ink is indigo-black #0b0510.
  • Don't use ctx.filter or shadowBlur per frame.
  • Don't make the light fields small and noisy. They are hundreds of units across.
  • Don't add a directional sun shadow. Shadows are only soft contact blobs.

Implementation recipe

  1. Set up a world-unit camera (360 units tall). Compute K = clamp(round(hPx / 360 × 1.3 × 4) / 4, 2.5, 5).
  2. Implement the toolkit: spline, limb(pts, w0, w1), curlPts/curl, leaf, ell, circ, poly, star, cut, pumpkinShape (or your own primitive bulb) and glowFill.
  3. Implement mk() (bake at K, feet at origin, ink fill), flip, tint, rimSpr (8 stamps + blur) and glowSpr.
  4. Design the main character as a puppet: torso and cloak, jointed limbs with curl tips, a distinctive head with one emissive feature. Bake 6 walk + 4 idle frames with rim copies.
  5. Design each creature class with a unique protruding feature and one emissive colour. Bake 4–6 frames each, plus an impact rim and torn shards.
  6. Write the ground shader: one warped-noise scalar → 4 threshold bands → edge-to-core colour → light bleed near the seam → optional ripples → paper grain → seam line. Use a 4-unit sample grid with bilinear interpolation, and render chunks in a generator.
  7. Draw the lace layer: seam beads projected onto sd = 0, then field-specific ink decals. Composite it at 80 %.
  8. Bake props as silhouettes, scatter them by field (warm props on amber and crimson, reeds on teal) and y-sort them with the characters.
  9. Per frame: draw the ground, contact shadows, the main character's spotlight, y-sorted sprites (rim 'lighter' first, then ink), additive emissive halos, particles and numbers, then the theatre frame.
  10. Effects: additive flares, sparks, embers and trails; ink paper scraps; torn-shard break-ups.
  11. Build the HUD with ink plates, gold hairline rings, glowing gradient bars and Cinzel numbers.

// Tapered cut-paper limb along a Catmull-Rom-smoothed polyline (the basic building block).
function spline(p, sub) {
  const o = [];
  for (let i = 0; i < p.length - 1; i++) {
    const p0 = p[i - 1] || p[i], p1 = p[i], p2 = p[i + 1], p3 = p[i + 2] || p[i + 1];
    for (let k = 0; k < sub; k++) {
      const t = k / sub, t2 = t * t, t3 = t2 * t;
      o.push([0, 1].map((j) => 0.5 * (2 * p1[j] + (-p0[j] + p2[j]) * t + (2 * p0[j] - 5 * p1[j] + 4 * p2[j] - p3[j]) * t2 + (-p0[j] + 3 * p1[j] - 3 * p2[j] + p3[j]) * t3)));
    }
  }
  o.push(p[p.length - 1]);
  return o;
}
function limb(c, pts, w0, w1, sub) {
  const s = pts.length > 2 ? spline(pts, sub || 5) : pts, n = s.length, L = [], R = [];
  for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
    const a = s[Math.max(0, i - 1)], b = s[Math.min(n - 1, i + 1)];
    let dx = b[0] - a[0], dy = b[1] - a[1]; const d = Math.hypot(dx, dy) || 1; dx /= d; dy /= d;
    const w = (w0 + (w1 - w0) * (i / (n - 1))) * 0.5;
    L.push([s[i][0] - dy * w, s[i][1] + dx * w]); R.push([s[i][0] + dy * w, s[i][1] - dx * w]);
  }
  c.beginPath(); c.moveTo(L[0][0], L[0][1]);
  for (let i = 1; i < n; i++) c.lineTo(L[i][0], L[i][1]);
  for (let i = n - 1; i >= 0; i--) c.lineTo(R[i][0], R[i][1]);
  c.closePath(); c.fill();
  c.beginPath(); c.arc(s[0][0], s[0][1], w0 / 2, 0, Math.PI * 2); c.fill();
}
// Spiral that tightens towards its end – every tip in this style finishes in one.
function curlPts(x, y, a, len, turn, n = 12) {
  const p = [[x, y]]; let h = a;
  for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
    const t = i / n; h += (turn * (0.25 + 1.75 * t)) / n;
    const s = (len / n) * (1.35 - 0.85 * t);
    x += Math.cos(h) * s; y += Math.sin(h) * s; p.push([x, y]);
  }
  return p;
}

// Emissive fill (carved face / eyes / lantern pane): gradient + baked bloom. K = px per world unit.
const FACE = [[0, '#fffbe2'], [0.35, '#ffd65e'], [0.75, '#ff9a22'], [1, '#ff6a10']];
function glowFill(c, path, cx, cy, r, stops, bloom, bloomCol, K) {
  c.save();
  const g = c.createRadialGradient(cx, cy, 0, cx, cy, r);
  stops.forEach((s) => g.addColorStop(s[0], s[1]));
  c.fillStyle = g;
  c.shadowColor = bloomCol; c.shadowBlur = bloom * K;   // pass 1: bloom halo
  c.beginPath(); path(c); c.fill();
  c.shadowBlur = 0;                                     // pass 2: crisp shape on top
  c.beginPath(); path(c); c.fill();
  c.restore();
}
// Hole in the paper: the floor light shows through.
function cut(c, fn) { c.save(); c.globalCompositeOperation = 'destination-out'; c.fillStyle = '#000'; fn(); c.restore(); }
// Rim of light around a baked silhouette (main character aura, impact flash). Add it with 'lighter' BEFORE the ink sprite.
function tint(src, col) {
  const c = document.createElement('canvas'); c.width = src.width; c.height = src.height;
  const x = c.getContext('2d'); x.drawImage(src, 0, 0);
  x.globalCompositeOperation = 'source-atop'; x.fillStyle = col; x.fillRect(0, 0, c.width, c.height);
  return c;
}
function rimSprite(src, color, rad, K) {          // rad in world units: main character 1.3, small creature 1.2, large creature 1.6
  const t = tint(src, color), d = Math.max(1, rad * K * 0.55);
  const tmp = document.createElement('canvas'); tmp.width = src.width; tmp.height = src.height;
  const tc = tmp.getContext('2d');
  for (let i = 0; i < 8; i++) { const a = (i / 8) * Math.PI * 2; tc.drawImage(t, Math.cos(a) * d, Math.sin(a) * d); }
  const out = document.createElement('canvas'); out.width = src.width; out.height = src.height;
  const oc = out.getContext('2d');
  oc.filter = `blur(${d.toFixed(1)}px)`; oc.drawImage(tmp, 0, 0); oc.filter = 'none';
  oc.globalAlpha = 0.6; oc.drawImage(tmp, 0, 0);
  return out;
}
// per frame: ctx.globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'; ctx.globalAlpha = 0.75; ctx.drawImage(rim, …);
//            ctx.globalCompositeOperation = 'source-over'; ctx.globalAlpha = 1; ctx.drawImage(ink, …);
// Ground light fields: one noise scalar f → four glowing bands. Per pixel, add bleed, seam line, grain.
const TH = [-0.95, -0.3, 0.35], BW = 0.075, HOT = [255, 240, 196];
const BANDS = [ { e: [44,116,140], c: [118,204,198] }, { e: [104,64,162], c: [182,130,220] },
                { e: [176,42,66], c: [242,106,82] },   { e: [234,122,44], c: [255,198,98] } ];
const ss = (a, b, x) => { let t = Math.min(1, Math.max(0, (x - a) / (b - a))); return t * t * (3 - 2 * t); };
function bandCol(i, f, gloss, o) {
  let d = i === 0 ? TH[0] - f : i === 3 ? f - TH[2] : Math.min(f - TH[i - 1], TH[i] - f); if (d < 0) d = 0;
  const B = BANDS[i], t = ss(0, 0.75, d) * (0.55 + 0.45 * gloss);
  for (let k = 0; k < 3; k++) o[k] = B.e[k] + (B.c[k] - B.e[k]) * t;
  if (i === 3) { const h = ss(0.9, 2.2, d) * (0.25 + 0.4 * gloss); for (let k = 0; k < 3; k++) o[k] += (HOT[k] - o[k]) * h; }
}
function fieldColor(f, gloss, out) {
  const s0 = ss(TH[0] - BW, TH[0] + BW, f), s1 = ss(TH[1] - BW, TH[1] + BW, f), s2 = ss(TH[2] - BW, TH[2] + BW, f);
  const w = [1 - s0, s0 - s1, s1 - s2, s2], t = [0, 0, 0]; out[0] = out[1] = out[2] = 0;
  for (let i = 0; i < 4; i++) if (w[i] > 0.001) { bandCol(i, f, gloss, t); for (let k = 0; k < 3; k++) out[k] += t[k] * w[i]; }
  for (let k = 0; k < 3; k++) out[k] *= 0.9 + 0.2 * gloss;
}
// sd = (f - nearestThreshold) / |grad f|  (world units to the seam), res = px per unit
function shadePixel(rgb, sd, res, grain) {
  const a = Math.abs(sd);
  if (a < 6) { const e = 1 - a / 6, k = e * e * 0.22; rgb[0] += (255 - rgb[0]) * k; rgb[1] += (240 - rgb[1]) * k; rgb[2] += (215 - rgb[2]) * k; }
  rgb[0] += grain; rgb[1] += grain * 0.9; rgb[2] += grain * 0.8;
  const l = (0.36 - a) * res + 0.5;
  if (l > 0) { const t = Math.min(1, l) * 0.85; rgb[0] += (22 - rgb[0]) * t; rgb[1] += (8 - rgb[1]) * t; rgb[2] += (28 - rgb[2]) * t; }
}

Adapting it to your project

  • Keep fixed:
    • the light-floor / ink-figure split;
    • ink #0b0510;
    • the limb / leaf / curl vocabulary with curled tips;
    • one emissive colour per character class;
    • the additive rim on the main character;
    • the seam-plus-lace ground;
    • the theatre frame.
  • Re-theme the fields. You may swap the four band colours as long as they stay luminous, saturated and ordered from cool to warm. A winter project could use ice-blue | lilac | rose | pale gold, and a desert project indigo | magenta | orange | sand. Keep the hot heart in the warmest band.
  • Re-theme the emissives. Use the warmest colour (gold/orange) for the main character and friendly lights, and cooler or acid colours for creatures.
  • New characters. Start from a bulb, a body or a veil, add one protruding identity feature (crown, horns, wings, antlers, hat), jointed limbs ending in curls, and an emissive face or carved mouth. Test the silhouette at 30 px tall in ink only.
  • New props. Reuse the toolkit: a trunk or post as a tapered limb, curls at every end, leaves at the tips, one destination-out hole for light, an optional emissive pane, and a contact-shadow width (sh) matching its footprint.
  • Mixing elements. The characters work on any bright, colourful ground as long as it is lighter than the ink; the light-field world works under any flat dark silhouettes; the theatre frame and vignette can close any dark-on-light scene.
  • Side-view projects. Use the same silhouettes and use the light fields as a horizontal backdrop (bands by height plus noise). This is the classic Reiniger look.
  • Other resolutions. Everything is in world units. Only K changes. Keep K ≥ 2.5 or the thin curls break up.

Performance notes

  • Baked once:
    • all frames, mirrors and rim and hurt-rim copies. A rim costs 8 drawImages + 1 blur filter at bake time only;
    • ghost halos (2 blurred passes);
    • torn shards, glows, about 20 prop sprites, icons;
    • number sprites (cached per value, cleared above 300 entries);
    • the theatre frame (per screen size).
  • Per frame: only drawImage with transforms, about 1–3 per figure (shadow, rim when flashing, sprite, glow), plus particles. Hundreds of creatures stay cheap. Cap impact-rim alpha and particle counts in big crowds.
  • The ground is the hot spot:
    • per 256-unit chunk: about a 70² noise grid (domain-warped fbm = 3 fbm calls per sample) plus a per-pixel loop at up to 2.5 px/unit (about 400k pixels);
    • compute it as a generator that yields every 2 rows and every 32 lace motifs, so chunks stream in without frame drops;
    • lace decals are vector paths into one offscreen canvas per chunk.
  • Check filter support (ctx.filter = 'blur(1px)'), and fall back to unblurred stamps on browsers without it.
