Harvest 16-Bit — Game Art Style Spec

Cozy SNES-era autumn farmland: warm hue-shifted pixel sprites, leaf-litter carpets, furrowed fields and wooden HUD panels.

A sunny autumn afternoon on the farm in true 16-bit pixel art. One art pixel equals one world unit; the low-res buffer is upscaled by an integer factor with nearest-neighbour sampling. Every sprite is painted by code into a tiny pixel grid: ellipses and tapered leaves whose sphere normals are lit by one shared top-left light, quantised into 3–5 step hue-shifted ramps (plum shadows, butter-yellow highlights), and finished with an automatic 1-px outline coloured from the sprite itself. The ground is a baked material map of three meadow greens, sunken dirt paths, ploughed fields and leaf carpets under round orange, red and gold trees. A warm soft-light grade and a plum vignette sell the late-afternoon mood. The module's cast is a pumpkin-headed main character with a carved glowing face, purple root creatures with leafy tops, pale ghosts and a lumpy root colossus; any chibi cast works.

When to use it

  • Good fits: top-down action games, farming and life sims, cozy adventure RPGs, seasonal (autumn, harvest, Halloween) projects, anything that wants "SNES classic" warmth with crowds of readable sprites.
  • Good because: sprites are small (20–30 px), outlined and saturated against a calmer ground, so even 100+ creatures on screen stay readable; everything is baked so it runs fast.
  • Bad fits: sci-fi/neon, grim horror (too friendly), painterly or high-res projects, very zoomed-out maps (the 1 px = 1 unit detail turns into noise), projects needing smooth rotation/scaling of sprites.

Rules

  • Every material is a hue-shifted ramp: shadows drift to purple/plum, light drifts to yellow/cream. Never darken with black.
  • Ground ≈ 70 % greens + browns; the saturated orange/red/gold comes from trees and leaf carpets.
  • Characters are warmer and more saturated than the ground: pumpkin orange, root purple, cream, ember yellow.
  • Ghosts are the only cool colour. Glow yellows only appear in faces, eyes, gems, lanterns, sparkles.
  • No pure black; pure white only in 1-px sparkle centres and the flash silhouette.

Typography

  • Pixelify Sans 400/500/600/700 — titles, card names, descriptions. https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Pixelify+Sans:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap

Rendering technique

  1. Buffer: pixel scale 1. Upscale factor up = max(1, round(displayH / 360)); buffer = ceil(W/up) × ceil(H/up). Draw the world into the buffer with imageSmoothingEnabled = false, then one drawImage to the display. The camera is snapped to whole art pixels (Math.round(cx * S) / S).
  2. Init (bake once): build every sprite with the Pix painter: main character (2 glow variants × 4 walk + 2 idle), small creature (2 colour variants × 4), ghost (4), large creature (4), white flash silhouettes (tint(..., '#ffffff')), dash afterimages (#ffc36a), 8 tree sprites, props, gems, seeds, sparkles, glow discs, shadows (widths 4–70), dust/vapour puffs, leaf particles, HUD icons (14×14 + outline = 16×16). Each gets a mirrored l copy.
  3. Pix painter: a w×h array of [r,g,b(,a)]. Primitives: set, rect, rows (text grid + char→colour map), Bresenham line, blob (ellipse with sphere normal callback), leaf (tapered blade with lit side and rib). canvas() adds a 1-px border and, for every empty pixel with filled 4-neighbours, writes mix(avg(neighbours), #24162a, 0.58 + 0.26 * luma) — the coloured outline.
  4. Light: LV = normalize(-0.5, -0.6, 0.62); l = n·LV; ramp index qi(l, thresholds) counts how many thresholds l exceeds. Typical thresholds [-0.22, 0.14, 0.5, 0.8] → 5 tones.
  5. Ground (per 256×256 chunk, generator that yields): noise fields on a 4-unit grid, bilinear sampled; material map; spur cleanup; tuft/trefoil/leaf stamps; colour pass with edge rules; macro tint; decals.
  6. Per frame (draw order): ground chunks → ground overlay (ring burst) → gems → plum shadows for all characters and props → y-sorted props/creatures/main character → orbiting objects → projectiles → particles → floating numbers → world overlay (falling and wind-blown leaves) → screen overlay (grade + vignette) → integer upscale.
  7. All positions are rounded; sprites are anchored at bottom centre (x - w/2, y - h + 1).

World and ground

The ground is an endless autumn farmland baked per 256×256-unit chunk at 1 px per unit (no tiles, no image assets). Material classes: GRASS 0, GOLD 1, CLOVER 2, SOIL 3, PATH 4. Grass classes are "high", soil and path "low" — that height difference drives the edge rules that make it look like 16-bit terrain.

  • Path: domain-warped Perlin pathN at 980-unit scale; distance to its zero line = |n| / |∇n|; half width 8.5 + macro * 6 units (≈ 17–29 units wide); jitter ±2.2·JA (a tileable 64×64 jitter) makes ragged edges. Inside: base #b08a5a, a rut line where |d - hw*0.42| < 1.05 (#9e7a50), edge #a4804f within 1.6 of the border, specks by hash: 5 % light #c4a06a, 3.5 % dark #987450, 1 % bright #d6b882. Pebbles and edge stones on top.
  • Fields: at most one rounded rectangle (corner radius 6) per 400×300 cell, 150–290 × 96–190 units, kept away from forests and paths. Furrows: fur[((Y + phase + wob) % 6)] — a 6-px stripe ramp, horizontal 70 % of the time; clods 3.5 % #b07a4c, 2.5 % #462a2c; a 3-px rim #734a35/#8d5d3d. Crops (sprouts or small root bulbs) on every ridge row (phase == 1) every 8 units. Fields may carry fences on the top/bottom edge and a scarecrow in 40 % of cases (see Props).
  • Meadows: clover where fbm(x/360) + fringe > 0.64, golden grass where fbm(x/430) > 0.555, else grass. A 52-unit mottle noise switches tone 6 (patch colour) and drives tuft density 0.012 + 0.45·smoothstep(.45,.72), so tufts gather in clumps and open grass stays calm.
  • Spur cleanup: remove 1-px spurs between height classes so every border is clean pixel art.
  • Edge rules (the "16-bit height" look): a grass pixel with low ground below → lip (dark), with low ground above → rim (bright), left/right → rim2/dk. A low pixel directly under grass → grass lip or sh (random 50/50), second row → checkerboard of sh2/sh (2-px dithered shadow), left of grass → sh2. Under grass, soil shadows are #3c2530/#513232, path shadows #755244/#8f6a4c.
  • Leaf carpets: density = forest field + one ellipse per tree (38×22 big, 31×18 small, q·1.8). Leaf stamps on a 3×3 grid survive when rank < (density·1.05 - 0.12)·(1 - inner) + inner·(0.08 + 0.55·smoothstep(.45,.7, mottle)) with inner = smoothstep(.55,.9, density): single leaves scatter along the carpet rim, the inside is a calm litter pool with leaf drifts only where the mottle noise is high. Above 0.62 density the gaps become litter colour, and the darker litter tone follows the clustered mottle mask (drifts), never per-pixel noise. The colour zone comes from the tree above it (TREE_ZONE = [3, 0, 2, 1]) and LEAFZ picks leaf sets per zone, so each carpet matches its canopy (red, orange, gold or turning). Without trees, use the litter zones #7c3531 #86412f #93582f #8d4a2d and leaf sets from the palette.
  • Macro tint: k = 0.955 + mv*0.09 from a 1100-unit noise, red ×(k + (mv-0.5)·0.03), blue ×(k - …) — warm and cool drift.

Characters

All characters are chibi sprites painted with the Pix painter (see Rendering technique): lit ellipse primitives quantised into hue-shifted ramps, details set pixel by pixel, the automatic coloured 1-px outline, light from the top-left, facing right with a mirrored copy for left, anchored at the feet and drawn at integer positions. Each creature type owns one hue family so it reads instantly against the green-brown ground.

Main character (pumpkin-headed figure, Pix(22, 30), 24×32 with outline, faces right, feet on row 29):

Front arm 2×3 sleeve + 2×2 glove #a4733e/#7e512e. Mustard scarf rows 16–17 (x 6–15) with a 3-px tail fluttering behind.

  • Pose table (bob, a, b, la, lb, hand, flap, hb, hx, face): body bob (+ = down), near/far leg x offsets and lifts, near-arm swing, scarf/cape lag, head offset relative to the body, head lean, face (normal, blink, hurt). Sprites bake lazily per pose key; walk, idle, dash and hurt poses are warmed at init.
  • Walk = 8 poses (contact, down, passing, up per leg): bob [0, 1, 0, -1, 0, 1, 0, -1], legs ±2 px with the passing foot lifted 2 px, the near arm swinging against the near leg, the cape and scarf tail trailing 0-2 px (flap) a step behind. The head uses the previous pose's body offset (hb), so it lands one frame late, and the stem leaf lifts when the head drops. Frame = floor(anim·1.3) & 7 (≈ 12 fps at walking speed).
  • Idle: breath on a 1.15 s beat (body 1 px down, head following 0.14 s later), blink every 3.6 s for 0.13 s (eyes become dim slits), cape/scarf stir every 4.6 s; every figure has its own phase offset.
  • Dash: 50 ms crouch wind-up (bob 2, legs wide, arm back) → stretch pose (legs split ±3, head leaning 1 px forward, scarf streaming) with 3 tinted afterimages → 120 ms skid (front boot forward, head leaning back).
  • Throw: the arm swings up and forward (hand 2) for ~90 ms after each shot.
  • Hurt: recoil pose (head tipped back, legs braced, squeezed eyes and an open mouth) alternating with its white silhouette at 8 Hz, then the 18 Hz blink.
  • Variants: a second set with the brighter glow ramp for flicker, a white flash silhouette and a #ffc36a silhouette (dash afterimage).

Small creature (purple root creature with a leafy top, 20×26): a bulb rx 7.6, ry 7.4 that tapers below ny > 0.1 (wf = 1 - 0.6·((ny-0.1)/0.9)^1.25), a wavy purple crown (zb = 0.02 + 0.12·sin(nx·8.5 + 1.3)) in the root purple ramp #2c1740 … #bf80c8, a blended seam band 0.13 deep, cream flesh #8a6458 … #fff6da, a 1-px root tip, three leaf() blades on top that sway ±1 px per frame, 2-px root legs #6e5040/#a2845e/#cdb282. Face: slanted #22122c brows, 2×2 glowing eyes, a frown with one cream fang. Two colour variants (purple / magenta #381634 … #d07e98). Frames shift the bulb ±0.5 px and bob 1 px, with a per-creature phase. A squash pose (bulb rx 8.8, ry 6, eyes squeezed shut) is used for ~120 ms after every hit (with a 2-px recoil) and for the disappear pop: white squash silhouette for 0.12 of the sequence, then the crumpled body holds before the burst.

Fast creature (ghost, 22×28): a head ellipse (6.6×7.4, alpha 242) and a sheet that streams back and waves (ccx = cx - 4t² + sin(f·π/2 + 4.2t)·1.9t), its lower half split into 3 tattered tails of lengths [0.88, 1.0, 0.76] rotating per frame; alpha fades 235 → 130 downward. Disappear: a white flash, then the sheet rises 15 units while its alpha runs out.

Large creature (root colossus, 52×58): a huge lumpy root bulb (rx 18, ry 17, radius bumped by sin(5a)/sin(11a)), purple crown #2e1832 … #8e5078 with crown leaves, ochre flesh #4e3028 … #ead08a, value noise ±0.11 on the light, moss speckles (lit top pixel) on crown and shoulders, a heavy brow shadow (−2 tones), black-plum cracks #24121e, glowing eye sockets, a 12-px jagged ember maw, a glowing chest crack, two 19-step root arms with knots and root fingers, 7-px gnarled stump legs.

Props

Props use the same painter, lit ramps, top-left light and automatic coloured outline as the characters. Each stands on a plum pixel shadow (see Atmosphere and lighting), gets a mirrored copy for variety and is y-sorted together with the characters. Materials: bark #3a2228 #5a3428 #7c4c32 #9e6c44, wood #3e2420 #64402a #8c5e36 #b8844c #d8aa6a, straw #7a5a2a … #faeaa0, foliage #22402c #356432 #4f8c3a #78b246.

  • Trees (56×65 small / 68×80 big, canopy radius 21 / 26): 13 overlapping blobs (a centre blob 0.8·R, a ring of 9 at 0.5–0.6·R, 3 inner blobs raised by z 6), edges wobbled by sin(7a)/sin(13a); per pixel the blob with the highest sqrt(r²-d²) + z wins and supplies the normal; light ×0.85 + 0.1, minus a top→bottom falloff 0.24, plus value noise ±0.15; quantised with thresholds [-0.12, 0.2, 0.47, 0.73] into a 5-tone canopy ramp (orange, red, gold or turning — see the palette). The bark trunk (half width 3.1/3.7) widens into a root flare in the bottom 20 %, forks into two branches below the canopy, and gets a dark canopy shadow band on its upper part. 4 ramps × 2 sizes = 8 tree sprites. Each tree stands on a leaf carpet in its own colour (see World and ground). The canopy is drawn at 60 % opacity when the main character walks behind it.
  • Hay bales: square 26×19 (lit top face 6 rows with diagonal straw strokes, darker front, two twine bands #c8643e at x 7 and 18 with #6a2a26/#9a4430 on the front, stray straw pixels) or round 28×20 (a lying cylinder in 4 straw tones with a spiral end on the left).

Effects and particles

All effects are pixel sprites or 1-px rings at integer positions — no strokes, no blur.

  • Projectile: 5×3 seed ['.cmm.', 'chmme', '.mee.'] (c #fff6dc, m #eedcaa, e #c9a874, h #fff, outline #5e3a2a), 4 rotations by spin; trail = 1-px #fff2c8 dots at −4 (α .55) and −6 (α .28) units.
  • Impact: the struck sprite shows its white flash silhouette while flash > 0.45; a 3-frame sparkle (5×5 plus #ffe9a0/#ffc84a/#ffffff → 3×3 → 1 px); 55 % chance of a leaf fluttering off (gravity 22, drag 1.5); ghosts emit vapour puffs instead; the large creature throws ochre chunks.
  • Burst: 8 (large creature 16) square chunks (2–3 px, 1-px highlight hi) in the sprite's own ramp colours, gravity 200; 3–6 leaves; 2–5 dust puffs; a 1-px ring #fff4d0 expanding from r 3 to 11 (large 6 → 22) over 0.22 s. Ghosts burst into 7 vapour puffs + a pale ring. The main character's burst uses orange pumpkin chunks #de6e2c/#f59a3c with #ffc76c highlights and a ring #ffb36b.
  • Rise from soil: creatures emerge with the sprite cropped from the top (outQuad) over a 12×3 dirt mound ['....hhll....', '..hlllmmmd..', '.llmmmmmdddd'] with flying dirt chunks #6e4632/#9a663f. Ghosts fade in and sink 8 units with vapour puffs instead.
  • Dust / vapour puffs: lit pixel balls radius 1–5 (ramps #b89c7c #d2bc9c #e8dcc0 / #8aa2d2 #c4d8f0 #f0f8ff), α 0.42.
  • Collectibles: small gem 3×5 (.a. / aya / yao / aoo / .o., y #fff4b0, a #ffc23e, o #f08a2a, outline #6a2a1a); large gem 5×7 teal #5ce0c8/#2aa8a0/#1a6a70. Both bob 1 px on a 3 rad/s sine over a tiny pixel shadow, glow with a stepped disc pulsing 0.5 ± 0.15, sparkle every ~1.25 s and pop out on a 9-px arc. Picking one up shows 2 (large 6) sparkles + a spark.
  • Orbiting objects: 13×15 root-bulb lanterns with a carved glowing face circling the main character, each with a flickering stepped glow (see Atmosphere and lighting).
  • Ring burst: a squashed (half-height) 1-px #ffd25a ring r 6 → 40 around the main character, 14 radial sparkles, 6 leaves.
  • Dash smear: 3 afterimages tinted #ffc36a, spaced 7 units back, alpha 0.16/0.32/0.48, plus dust puffs.
  • Falling leaves: 2–3 px leaf frames (4 rotations) drift from visible canopies; 9 wind-blown leaves wrap across the view (16–27 units/s right, 7 units/s down).

Atmosphere and lighting

The mood is a warm late afternoon. One light from the top-left-front (normalize(-0.5, -0.6, 0.62)) is baked into every sprite ramp; the world itself has no blur, fog or smooth gradients.

  • Shadows: pixel ellipses w × round(w·0.36) in plum rgb(40,22,56), alpha 92 inside q ≤ 0.55, 62 outside — two hard steps, never blurred, never black. Widths: main character 14 (12 while dashing), small creature 14, ghost 10 (×0.55 alpha), large creature 32, big tree 50, small tree 40, offset +1…3 units right. Ground decals use single multiply shadow pixels (r*0.72, g*0.7, b*0.8).
  • Fake height: grass edges carry bright rims and dark lips; soil and paths get the 2-px dithered shadow band under grass (see World and ground).
  • Glows: additive ('lighter') discs with 3 hard alpha rings, never radial gradients: gem r 3 #ff9030 (.26/.06), large gem r 5 #40d0c0, lantern r 11 #ffa040 (.32/.1) flickering to r 13, main character face r 9 #ffb040. Glow colours only appear in carved faces, eyes, gems, lanterns and sparkles.
  • Transparency: ghosts are drawn at 86 % opacity; tree canopies drop to 60 % opacity when the main character is behind them.
  • Grade: a linear gradient rgba(255,190,110,.55) (top-left) → rgba(255,170,100,.18) → rgba(80,90,160,.42) (bottom-right) composited soft-light at α 0.5 — warm orange light, cool blue shade.
  • Vignette: a radial gradient white → rgb(150,110,150) (inner radius 0.35·min(W,H), outer 0.62·diagonal) composited multiply. Grade and vignette are pre-rendered once per buffer size and applied before the upscale.

Motion and animation

  • Main character: 8-pose walk (contact, down, passing, up × 2) at floor(anim·1.3) & 7, ≈ 12 fps at walking speed; arms counter-swing, the head lands one frame after the body, the cape and scarf tail lag a step, the stem leaf lifts when the head drops. Footstep dust on the two "down" poses.
  • Idle: breath 1.15 s (head 0.14 s behind), blink every 3.6 s, cape stir every 4.6 s; per-figure phase offsets.
  • Dash: 50 ms crouch → stretch pose + 3 tinted afterimages 7 units apart → 120 ms skid. Throw arm ~90 ms.
  • Small creature ≈ 6 fps (anim·1.6 + seed·4), large creature ≈ 2.6 fps (anim·1.2 + seed·4) with foot-fall dust, ghost 6 fps with an eased 8-step float at 5 fps — no plain sine bobs.
  • Hit: white silhouette while flash > 0.45, the squash pose and a 2-px recoil for ~120 ms.
  • Appear: roots climb out of a dirt mound (top rows revealed with outQuad) and throw soil chunks; ghosts rise 8 units while fading in with vapour puffs. Disappear: white squash pop → crumpled hold → burst of chunks, leaves, dust and a ring; ghosts flash and rise while thinning out.
  • Face glow flicker: noise(t·9) > 0.62 → brighter glow ramp. Lantern glow flickers every 7th tick.
  • Gems bob 1 px on a 3 rad/s sine; pop-up arc of 9 px when they appear. Floating numbers pop up 2–3 px for 0.08 s, then fade.
  • Everything moves in whole art pixels — no sub-pixel easing, no rotation or scaling of sprites (except the half-height squash of the ring burst).

Do and don't

Do

  • Render at 1 art px per world unit into a low-res buffer and upscale by an integer factor, smoothing off.
  • Paint sprites with lit primitives and quantised hue-shifted ramps; let the baker add the coloured outline.
  • Give the ground fake height with rims, lips and the 2-px dithered shadow band under grass edges.
  • Put leaf carpets in the tree's own colour under trees; mix three meadow greens with noise.
  • Use stepped additive glow discs and plum two-step shadows.
  • Keep pixel decals tiny (1–7 px) and give them 1-px multiply shadow pixels.
  • Finish with the warm soft-light grade and plum vignette.

Don't

  • Don't use canvas strokes, arcs, blur or gradients on sprites or ground.
  • Don't draw at fractional coordinates, rotate sprites or scale by non-integer factors.
  • Don't use black outlines or black shadows; shadows are plum (40,22,56) at 36 %/24 % alpha.
  • Don't light from more than one direction; every ramp assumes top-left light.
  • Don't leave flat ground — no tufts/specks/rims reads as a placeholder — but don't sprinkle leaves or tufts evenly either: calm pools and clumps, detail at the rims.
  • Don't animate with 2–4 near-identical frames or same-side arm and leg swings; don't let crowds share a phase.
  • Don't mix pixel scales (e.g. 2× sprites over 1× ground) or add neon colours outside the ghost/ice family.

Implementation recipe

  1. Set up a canvas; compute up = max(1, round(h / 360)) and a buffer ceil(w/up) × ceil(h/up).
  2. Port the Pix painter (set/rect/rows/line/blob/leaf/canvas with coloured outline) and lam/qi.
  3. Define your ramps (3–5 hue-shifted tones each) from the palette above.
  4. Write one pose-driven makeX(pose) function per character: legs first (far leg darker), body, arms, head, face. Bake the 8 walk poses plus idle, dash, hurt and squash poses lazily, mirror them, bake white flash silhouettes.
  5. Build the ground baker: noise fields → material map → stamps → colour pass with edge rules → decals. Cache it per 256-unit chunk (or once for a fixed screen).
  6. Bake props (trees with the blob canopy, fences, hay) and small sprites (gems, projectiles, sparkles, glows, shadows).
  7. Each frame: blit ground, shadows, y-sorted characters and props at integer positions, effects, leaves.
  8. Apply the soft-light grade and multiply vignette, then upscale the buffer with nearest-neighbour.
  9. Build the HUD in HTML/CSS with the wooden panel CSS and the two pixel fonts.

The shared light and the coloured-outline bake:

const hx = (s) => [parseInt(s.slice(1, 3), 16), parseInt(s.slice(3, 5), 16), parseInt(s.slice(5, 7), 16)];
const mixc = (a, b, t) => a.slice(0, 3).map((v, i) => Math.round(v + (b[i] - v) * t));
const INK = hx('#24162a');
const LV = (() => { const v = [-0.5, -0.6, 0.62], l = Math.hypot(...v); return v.map((a) => a / l); })();
const lam = (nx, ny, nz) => nx * LV[0] + ny * LV[1] + nz * LV[2];
const qi = (l, th) => { let i = 0; while (i < th.length && l > th[i]) i++; return i; };
const outlineOf = (c) => mixc(c, INK, 0.58 + 0.26 * ((c[0] * 0.3 + c[1] * 0.59 + c[2] * 0.11) / 255));

function bakeWithOutline(w, h, get /* (x,y) -> [r,g,b,a?] | null */) {
  const W = w + 2, H = h + 2, c = document.createElement('canvas'); c.width = W; c.height = H;
  const ctx = c.getContext('2d'), img = ctx.createImageData(W, H), d = img.data;
  for (let y = 0; y < H; y++) for (let x = 0; x < W; x++) {
    let col = get(x - 1, y - 1), a = 255;
    if (col) a = col.length > 3 ? col[3] : 255;
    else {
      const nb = [get(x - 2, y - 1), get(x, y - 1), get(x - 1, y - 2), get(x - 1, y)].filter(Boolean);
      if (!nb.length) continue;
      const avg = [0, 1, 2].map((i) => nb.reduce((s, q) => s + q[i], 0) / nb.length);
      col = outlineOf(avg); a = nb.reduce((s, q) => s + (q.length > 3 ? q[3] : 255), 0) / nb.length;
    }
    const o = (y * W + x) * 4; d[o] = col[0]; d[o + 1] = col[1]; d[o + 2] = col[2]; d[o + 3] = a;
  }
  ctx.putImageData(img, 0, 0);
  return c;
}

A lit pumpkin with ribs (the main character's head and the pumpkin props), painted into a grid:

function pumpkin(w, h, cx, cy, rx, ry, creases) {
  const ramp = ['#74282a', '#b0462a', '#de6e2c', '#f59a3c', '#ffc76c'].map(hx);
  const grid = new Array(w * h).fill(null), idx = new Map();
  const set = (x, y, c) => { if (x >= 0 && y >= 0 && x < w && y < h) grid[y * w + x] = c; };
  for (let y = Math.floor(cy - ry) - 1; y <= Math.ceil(cy + ry); y++) for (let x = Math.floor(cx - rx) - 1; x <= Math.ceil(cx + rx); x++) {
    const nx = (x + 0.5 - cx) / rx, ny = (y + 0.5 - cy) / ry, dd = nx * nx + ny * ny;
    if (dd > 1) continue;
    const k = qi(lam(nx, ny, Math.sqrt(1 - dd)), [-0.22, 0.14, 0.5, 0.8]);
    idx.set(x + ',' + y, k); set(x, y, ramp[k]);
  }
  for (let y = Math.floor(cy - ry); y <= Math.ceil(cy + ry); y++) {          // 1-px rib curves
    const ny = (y + 0.5 - cy) / ry; if (Math.abs(ny) >= 0.92) continue;
    for (const c of creases) {
      const x = Math.floor(cx + c * rx * Math.sqrt(1 - ny * ny)), k = idx.get(x + ',' + y);
      if (k !== undefined) set(x, y, ramp[Math.max(0, k - 1)]);
    }
  }
  return bakeWithOutline(w, h, (x, y) => (x < 0 || y < 0 || x >= w || y >= h ? null : grid[y * w + x]));
}

function rowsSprite(rows, pal, outlineHex) {
  const w = Math.max(...rows.map((r) => r.length)), h = rows.length;
  const get = (x, y) => (x < 0 || y < 0 || y >= h || !rows[y][x] || !pal[rows[y][x]] ? null : hx(pal[rows[y][x]]));
  const c = bakeWithOutline(w, h, get);

    const ctx = c.getContext('2d'), img = ctx.getImageData(0, 0, c.width, c.height), oc = hx(outlineHex);

      const o = (y * c.width + x) * 4; if (img.data[o + 3] && !get(x - 1, y - 1)) img.data.set(oc, o);
    }
    ctx.putImageData(img, 0, 0);

}

function shadowSprite(w) { // plum ellipse, two hard alpha steps
  const h = Math.max(2, Math.round(w * 0.36)), c = document.createElement('canvas'); c.width = w; c.height = h;
  const ctx = c.getContext('2d'), img = ctx.createImageData(w, h);
  for (let y = 0; y < h; y++) for (let x = 0; x < w; x++) {
    const nx = (x + 0.5 - w / 2) / (w / 2), ny = (y + 0.5 - h / 2) / (h / 2), q = nx * nx + ny * ny;
    if (q <= 1) img.data.set([40, 22, 56, q > 0.55 ? 62 : 92], (y * w + x) * 4);
  }
  ctx.putImageData(img, 0, 0);
  return c;
}

// GRASS 0, GOLD 1, CLOVER 2 are "high"; SOIL 3, PATH 4 are "low"
if (m <= CLOVER) {
  const gp = GP[m];

Adapting it to your project

  • Keep fixed: 1 px = 1 unit and integer upscale; the shared light vector; quantised ramps with hue shift; the auto coloured outline; plum shadows; the ground edge rules; stepped glows; the grade + vignette.
  • May change: the cast (any chibi character ~20–30 px; large creatures up to ~52×58), the crops and props, the season. For spring/summer swap the canopy ramps to greens and blossoms and the leaf carpets to petals; for winter swap meadows for snow ramps but keep the same edge rules.
  • Palette swaps: replace whole ramps, not single colours; keep 3–5 tones, keep the hue drift (shadow → purple, light → yellow), keep the outline target #24162a.
  • New creatures: give each its own hue family and silhouette; reuse blob, leaf and carve.
  • Mixing elements: the characters work on other worlds as long as those are pixel art at the same scale; this world under other characters needs them pixelled at 1 px per unit with a coloured outline.
  • Other views: side-view scenes work if you keep the top-left light; higher, zoomed-out cameras need lower tuft density and sprites of at least 16 px.
  • Resolution: change the visible world height (e.g. 270 instead of 360) rather than the pixel scale.

Performance notes

  • All sprites, frames, mirrored copies and flash silhouettes are baked once in init; per frame only integer drawImage blits into the small buffer plus one upscale.
  • The ground is the only heavy part: per 256×256 chunk several fbm fields on a 4-unit grid (bilinear), per-pixel SDF/material work and stamp passes. Build it in a generator that yields every 32 rows and cache chunks (the module keeps ~90).
  • Field and tree placement uses cached per-cell lookups (Map, cleared above 6000/8000 entries).
  • Overlays are pre-rendered per buffer size; leaf particles are 2–3 px sprites.
  • Budget: the starter scene renders in about 2 ms per frame at 960×540 after its one-time bake.
